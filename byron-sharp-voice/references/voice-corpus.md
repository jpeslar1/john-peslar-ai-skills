# Byron Sharp Voice Corpus

Research date: 2026-10-19

The excerpts below are short quotations from public posts, articles, and talks, included under fair use for style analysis and attribution. They are third-party content and NOT covered by this repository's MIT license.

**Provenance note.** Items 1-35 are verbatim and written by him. Items 1-27 are copied from the full bodies of posts on his own WordPress blog (byronsharp.wordpress.com, October 2010 to September 2020), pulled through the wordpress.com public API; his double spaces, spaced question marks, and typos (amatuer, tinniest, gleam) are kept. Items 3 and 4 come from a post he describes as "a footnote from How Brands Grow", so they are the only book text in the corpus. Items 28-35 are his own X posts (@ProfByron, 2019-2021), read from the X syndication endpoint. Items 36-39 are DOCUMENTED: direct quotes attributed to him by a journalist in a December 2015 In The Black (CPA Australia) interview, read from a Wayback capture; they are the only spoken sample. Nothing here comes from a quote aggregator. How Brands Grow itself, his paywalled WARC responses, and his podcast appearances were not sampled, and his current LinkedIn activity was not searched.

**Punctuation note.** Several excerpts preserve spaced en dashes because they are verbatim quotations (items 7, 14, 16, 18, 38, 39), and many of his post titles use an en dash as a colon ("True Brand Loyalty – it doesn't matter", "Programmatic – don't believe the hype"). The operator's copy rule still bans em and en dashes in anything generated in this voice; see the Punctuation Override in SKILL.md.

## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~17 words in blog prose, with a 3-6 word verdict sentence or fragment closing most paragraphs ("Dangerously simplistic.", "that's it.", "Wow."); X posts run 1-3 sentences of 8-15 words.
- One-line paragraphs: roughly a third of blog paragraphs are a single sentence, almost always the dismissal after two to four sentences of exposition; on X the whole post is often one sentence.
- Hook-type frequency across the 39 excerpts: orthodoxy restated then negated ("The mantra within marketing and R&D departments is...", "So says Seth...") ~35%; flat law stated as fact ("Search is physical availability.") ~25%; rhetorical question aimed at the reader's gullibility ("Why is charging too much seen as a indicator of marketing strength ?") ~20%; analogy to another science or to history (chaos theory, Roman chickens, Einstein) ~15%; the evidence demand ("what's the evidence on loyalty programs?") ~5%.
- Parenthetical asides doing the sarcasm: about 1 in 3 excerpts ("(cesspool?)", "(yes, that's capitalism)", "(and snake-oil selling)", "(and marketing academics)").
- Scare quotes around rivals' terms: about 1 in 4 excerpts ('theory', 'Big Data fallacy', 'explain', "golden households", "super consumers", 'true loyalty' in the research notes).
- Exclamation marks: about 1 in 8 excerpts, always on a mocking line ("lemmings running for the cliff!", "No one asks for the ROI of the finance department!"). Emoji: zero. Hashtags: effectively zero (#marketing / #pseudoscience noted, none in the sample).
- ALL CAPS: rare, for a shouted verdict in the research notes ("THEY BRAND THAT'S ALL"); none in the 39 excerpts. Profanity: zero; contempt is carried by twaddle, nonsense, hype, pseudoscience, snake-oil, wimpy, shoddy, gullible.
- Numbers: sparse. He cites patterns and laws (Double Jeopardy, penetration, repertoire) rather than figures; the only numerals in the sample are "1% inspiration, 99% perspiration" and dates. Do not stat-stack in this voice.
- Hedges: used as scientific precision, not softening ("(potentially)", "tend to", "much of the purpose", "Sometimes we get lucky, but generally"); conclusions themselves are stated flat ("It's not a sensible exercise.").
- Typos and formatting left unpolished: double spaces after full stops, a space before question marks, misspellings kept; he signs some posts "Professor Byron Sharp" with the month and year.
- Favorite openers: "It takes only a moment of thought to realise that..."; "Contrary to popular belief..."; "The mantra within ... is..."; "Have you ever been to a marketing conference and not heard..."; a title that is itself the contrarian claim.
- Favorite closers: a one-line verdict or fragment ("Dangerously simplistic.", "It's as if chaos theory taught us nothing at all.", "First discover, then seek explanations."); an "X is not Y" law; a pointer to the evidence ("see Romaniuk & Sharp on mental availability").
- Spoken register (In The Black, journalist transcription): looser and more anecdotal ("the emperor has no clothes", "attached to your hip pocket", "do some research"), the same contempt for fashion over evidence, more direct quotation of the imagined opponent.

# Ross Simmonds Voice Corpus

Research date: 2026-10-19

The excerpts below are short quotations from public posts, articles, and talks, included under fair use for style analysis and attribution. They are third-party content and NOT covered by this repository's MIT license.

**Provenance note.** All 29 items are verbatim. Items 1-16 are copied from the full post bodies returned by the WordPress REST API of his own blog, rosssimmonds.com (2024-2026, each dated by the API). Items 17-21 are his own X posts (@TheCoolestCool): root tweets were fetched and dated via fxtwitter, and mid-thread tweets came from Thread Reader unrolls of the same threads (marked inline; they rest on Thread Reader's copy, not an individual re-fetch). Items 22-26 are LinkedIn posts from October 2026 whose permalinks render logged out; text is from the page meta description and dates from JSON-LD. Items 27-29 are from a Foundation Labs article on his agency's site (foundationinc.co) bylined to him; it is first-person but written for the agency blog, so treat it as his agency register rather than his personal blog register. Nothing here comes from a quote aggregator. His book Create Once, Distribute Forever (2022) was not accessed, and no spoken sample was collected, so the corpus is written-voice only. Emoji inside quotes are his; Canadian spellings (favourite, honour) noted in the research are his.

**Punctuation note.** One excerpt (item 1) preserves an em dash because it is a verbatim quotation ("Content isn't king—distribution is"); it is the only em dash in the corpus. His habitual beat mark is the ellipsis (items 4, 7, 12, 14, 15, 24, 25, 26), kept as written. The operator's copy rule still bans em and en dashes in anything generated in this voice; see the Punctuation Override in SKILL.md.

## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~10 words in blog essays and ~12 on X and LinkedIn; one-word or two-word lines recur as beats ("Folks.", "Hours.", "Quietly…", "Huge mistake.").
- One-line paragraphs: roughly 55% of blog paragraphs are a single sentence or fragment; the longest paragraphs in the sample run three sentences.
- Hook-type frequency across the 29 excerpts: stat or stark claim followed by a hard pivot ~30% ("90% of content… This is a shame.", "That ratio is backwards."); personal story or reflection ~20% (du rags, the 72 hours, the annual review); consensus flip ~15% ("Content is king… Content isn't king"); reader question ~15% ("Know what blows my mind?", "You know that moment when…"); hot-take rebuttal ~10% ("The hot take of the quarter"); borrowed-format parody ~10% ("5 stages of grief (but make it B2B marketing edition)").
- Triads and anaphora: present in about a fifth of excerpts ("Three different categories. Three different buying contexts. One consistent source."; "My # 1 class. My # 1 mentor… My # 1 professor…").
- Ellipses as a beat: 8 of 29 excerpts ("It's just… obvious.", "Truly an honor…", "So to be honest...").
- ALL-CAPS subheads inside threads: 1 of 29 ("HAVE THE COURAGE TO BE UNFOLLOWED"); no other caps emphasis.
- Emoji: 2 in 29 excerpts (👇 and ✌🏿), both on LinkedIn; zero in blog essays. Hashtags: zero. Exclamation marks: zero.
- Profanity: zero in the sample; the research notes "distribute like hell" as his strongest phrase.
- Hedges: rare and personal rather than qualifying ("So to be honest...", "I've always believed", "For me:"); claims themselves are flat ("Distribution is now the only moat that matters.").
- Numbers: ratios and round counts used as proof of the distribution thesis (90% of content under 50 visits, 80/20 backwards, 10+ years, 47 brand websites, 10 mentions > 1,000 citations, 72 hours, 20 years); no decimals.
- Favorite openers: "You've probably heard it a million times:", "Here's my golden rule:", "Know what blows my mind?", "The hot take of the quarter:", "Folks.", "This is the hill I will always stand on."
- Favorite closers: the motto ("Create once. Distribute forever."), "Keep moving. Keep distributing.", a "PS:" or "let's talk" pitch to Foundation or Distribution.ai, a reader question ("Whatcha think?"), and "More to come. I promise."
- Spoken vs written: no spoken sample. Blog essays are longer and story-first; X and LinkedIn are clipped, list-driven, and end on a CTA; the Foundation Labs byline is the same voice with more agency framing.

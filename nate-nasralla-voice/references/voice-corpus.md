# Nate Nasralla Voice Corpus

Research date: 2026-10-19

The excerpts below are short quotations from public posts, articles, and talks, included under fair use for style analysis and attribution. They are third-party content and NOT covered by this repository's MIT license.

**Provenance note.** All 31 items are verbatim and written by him, but they come from two registers that should not be blended. Items 1-25 are copied from the full text of 16 of his own LinkedIn posts (profile natenasralla, 2022-2026, nine of them from the month before the research date), fetched logged-out from each permalink with his slash lists, arrows, asterisks, and apostrophe plurals intact; this is his native voice. Items 26-30 are from three bylined fluint.io blog essays (June-July 2026, 'Nate Nasralla, Co-Founder @ Fluint'); they are his byline but noticeably more edited, more polished, and tied to Fluint products (Loop, Olli Mesa), so treat them as his edited register and quote them as essays, not as posts. Item 31 is his own landing-page copy for the book Selling With, not book-interior text. Nothing here comes from a quote aggregator. No excerpt of Selling With (2023) or Brief & Brilliant could be obtained, and no podcast transcript was found, so there is no spoken sample.

**Punctuation note.** Two LinkedIn excerpts preserve em dashes because they are verbatim quotations (items 19 and 22), item 14 preserves a trailing unicode ellipsis, and items 8, 11, and 22 keep his '→' arrows, which are part of the voice. The 2026 fluint.io essays use colons and periods instead of dashes. The operator's copy rule still bans em and en dashes in anything generated in this voice; see the Punctuation Override in SKILL.md.

## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~9 words in posts (mean ~10); the fluint.io essays average 12-15 words with the same two-beat contrast rhythm.
- One-line paragraphs: roughly three quarters of post lines are one sentence or one list item; a post is 6-15 short lines.
- Hook-type frequency across the 25 post excerpts: framing sentence plus colon then a list ~35% ('Here are 2 phrases...', 'here's a shortlist of 6 things'), flat contrast claim ~25% ('Average reps try... Standout reps try...'), borrowed authority with credit (Ogilvy, Doug May, a Fortune 100 buyer) ~15%, a mistake or myth named ~15%, personal scene ~10%.
- List notation: '1/ 2/ 3/' slashes, '(1) (2)' parentheticals, '→' arrows, and '-' hyphens; bullets are never '•'. Seven arrows in 31 items, concentrated in items 8, 11, and 22.
- Asterisks for emphasis: about ten in the sample (*not*, *different*, *really fast*, *as your process*, *Target and Sell to Trainwrecks*); caps used for stretch and pacing ('SOME ARE LOUD', 's l o w') rather than stress.
- Parenthetical asides: in about a third of posts, qualifying or softening '(Vs. an early, ugly doc...)', '(Remember nothing sticks to smooth surfaces.)', '(Security, Legal, IT, and Procurement)'.
- Questions: 26 across 31 items; nearly every post ends on one ('Anyone else got # 7 to add?', 'You can do that, right?', 'So do buyers accelerate your timeframe if you slow it down?'). Exclamation marks: zero.
- Hedges: light and deliberate ('IMO', 'probably', 'I mean, if...', 'Definitely not. / Definitely.'); confidence arrives as a worked number, not a boast.
- Numbers: specific and mathematical (Velocity = (# Opps x ACV x Win Rate %) / Cycle Time, 4.5/5, 20% of the trial activity to dispel 80% of the doubt, as much as 6X, $15M of IT spend, 100+ paid customers, 95% of sales books unread); about two per post.
- Emoji: zero. Hashtags: zero. Profanity: zero; the strongest word is 'total bummer'.
- Favorite openers: 'Here are N...' or 'Here's...' plus a colon; 'The single, biggest mistake...'; 'This is the best analogy for...'; 'You know what's a total bummer?'; 'I've never met a "normal" enterprise executive.'
- Favorite closers: an open question or a gentle dare ('Give 'em a shot this week.', 'why wait?'); essays close on a two-beat aphorism ('Coverage is an open loop. Your revenue should be a closed one.').
- Spoken vs written: no transcript sampled; his own description of his keynote style (items 5-6: slides for toddlers, switch speed and tone, land one big idea) is the only evidence of spoken cadence.

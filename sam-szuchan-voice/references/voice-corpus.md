## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length (2026 register): ~14 words, with a wide spread: a 25-35 word evidence sentence is followed by a 2-5 word verdict ("Same data. Different lens.", "It reroutes.", "Not as a joke. As a career.").
- Paragraphs (2026 register): 2-4 sentences, not one line per sentence; one-line paragraphs are reserved for the thesis turn or the final line and make up roughly 20% of a post. The 2023 register is the opposite: nearly every line is its own paragraph.
- Hook-type frequency across the 31 current-era excerpts: hard, specific, slightly absurd fact in sentence one ("John Kerry and Dick Cheney said the exact same sentence, two months apart, in 2004.", "49 tweets between 3 and 4 a.m.") ~55%, flat contradiction stated as settled ("It is also the most consequential infrastructure project of the 21st century.") ~20%, the reader's blind spot named ("Western analysis struggles with this because...") ~15%, aphorism ("Structure decides before anyone chooses.") ~10%. Zero question hooks, zero "Here's how".
- Hook-type frequency across the 7 secondary-era excerpts: problem statement then "most advice is generic" then "So I'm stepping in." or "So I created..." ~70%; a "(Actually)" title device appears in 3 of the 7 post titles.
- Numbers: exact and sourced, never rounded for effect: $839 billion, 88%, $1,400 a month, 40,000 posts, 49 tweets, one every 80 seconds, one million to twenty million, 23-year-old, fifty cents a month; about 1 in 2 current-era excerpts carries a figure.
- Emoji: zero in all 39 excerpts. Hashtags: zero. Repost or follow asks: zero in the 2026 register; one "Check it out below" style funnel in the 2023 register per the research notes.
- Exclamation marks: zero in the 2026 register outside quoted speech; 3 in the 2023 register ("get it while it's hot!", "can you believe it!"). Profanity: zero.
- First person: appears in roughly 1 in 5 current-era excerpts and only to show method ("I wanted to understand why.", "Then I looked at the numbers.", "I tweeted about the Anthropic JD"); never opens a post. The 2023 register opens on "I" freely ("So I'm stepping in.", "I'm launching my services").
- Hedges: near zero. He states the structural reading flat ("Nobody designed this system. Nobody voted on it.") and refuses the moral reading explicitly ("this is an analytic cop-out").
- Semicolons and two-sentence contrasts replace the "It's not X, it's Y" cliche: "This isn't team bonding; it's an obedience test", "That distance was not decoration. It was load-bearing." About 1 in 4 current-era excerpts uses the pattern.
- Favorite openers (2026): a dated or located fact ("In March 2024, a 23-year-old K-pop idol...", "In Seoul, they call...", "On Xiaohongshu..."); a count ("One-third of all GoFundMe money..."). Favorite openers (2023): "Most LinkedIn advice is the same:", "You can have the world's greatest content, but...".
- Favorite closers (2026): one quiet line that re-angles the post with no advice ("Someone is paid to tell you that.", "The ballet companies, meanwhile, still need to sell tickets.", "The phone is where the trillion dollars lives."). Favorite closers (2023): "Keep going.", "Check it out below:", a PS with a wink.
- Spoken register (ASR sample, 2024): loose and filler-heavy ("like", "um"), tells the zero-likes-to-700-likes arc anecdotally; the written version of the same arc is clipped ("The zeros became singles, and the singles became dozens."). Use written only.
- Spoken register, longer sample (items 101-110, one March 2024 interview, documented-grade; a between-eras recording): calmer and more hedged than either written era. Answers open with a framing clause ("How I approach it fundamentally", "I can definitely see that."), sentences run 20-40 words, "literally" appears 14 times in his turns, and he concedes limits openly ("I'm a remote work advocate, but I'm not a fanatic."), which the zero-hedge 2026 essays never do. The 2026 habit of a short verdict after a long sentence shows only occasionally ("It's immediate, but it's very insidious"), there is no profanity, and no 2023 hook formula. Treat this as how he talks, not as a template for either written register.

## What the corpus rests on

Five of thirty-three excerpts are strictly verbatim, written by her: the full text of public LinkedIn posts from 2019 to 2022, dated from each post's activity ID. The other twenty-eight are documented-grade and fall into three groups: eight Instagram captions quoted by press (Fortune, Shark Tank Blog, Parade, Yahoo), fourteen YouTube auto-captions of her own Business Unusual podcast monologues and Q&A answers (her own words, spoken and scripted), and six passages from a 2013 Fortune as-told-to memoir transcribed by a journalist. Nothing comes from a quote aggregator. The spoken and scripted register is covered with high confidence; the written register rests on five short posts plus press-quoted captions and is therefore inferred. Confidence in wording is high for items 1-5, moderate for 6-13 and 28-33 (quoted or transcribed by third parties), and moderate for 14-27 (ASR, with one numeric slip corrected).

## Pattern notes

### 1. Maxim first, then the story that earned it
Her strongest units are 8-14 word rules in plain words, immediately backed by a personal anecdote carrying a dollar figure. The Ghostwriter playbook interviews for the user's own maxim and number before writing anything.

### 2. The quoted insult, then revenge as fuel
"You'll never succeed without me" appears in the 2013 memoir, the 2019 LinkedIn post, and the podcast; it is always quoted, and it always converts into "prove them wrong." The Objection Handling Expert playbook reframes every objection as an insult, and every insult as advice in reverse.

### 3. Named-listener verdict
Podcast answers open with the caller's name and the verdict, then the story. Replies, DMs, and objection responses follow the same order: name, verdict, proof, order.

### 4. Don't take the first offer
The Merrill Lynch $50,000 backout and the $20 million to $66 million counter are her negotiation proof. The Sales Closer playbook states the number once, flatly, and treats the first offer as information rather than the deal.

### 5. People-reading over planning
Hire for attitude, fire chronic complainers, send flowers to the competitor's agents, love your people so they love you back. Management and sales advice always routes through reading the person, never through a process.

### 6. Announced repetition and the imperative close
"I'm repeating that because there are no exceptions to that rule" marks the one rule that matters; every segment ends on an order. Both are used once per piece in the playbooks, and a draft without an imperative last line is off-voice.

# Barbara Corcoran Voice Corpus

Research date: 2026-10-18

The excerpts below are short quotations from public posts, articles, and talks, included under fair use for style analysis and attribution. They are third-party content and NOT covered by this repository's MIT license.

**Provenance note.** Only items 1-5 are strictly verbatim, written by her: the full text of five public LinkedIn posts (profile barbaracorcoran) that render without login, dates decoded from each post's activity ID. Everything else is DOCUMENTED. Items 6-13 are her own written Instagram captions (a letter to her 23-year-old self, a graduation-advice caption, a longevity caption) as quoted by Fortune, Shark Tank Blog, Parade, and Yahoo Entertainment; the captions themselves could not be fetched because Instagram is login-walled. Items 14-27 are YouTube auto-captions (ASR) of her own Business Unusual podcast, where she reads scripted first-person monologues and answers listener questions; these are her own words, spoken, with punctuation added and no words changed, and one numeric caption slip is corrected and flagged inline. Items 28-33 are a first-person as-told-to memoir piece transcribed by a Fortune journalist in 2013. Nothing here comes from a quote aggregator. Her two highest-volume written channels, X (@BarbaraCorcoran) and Instagram, were not sampled directly, so her written voice here rests on five short LinkedIn posts plus press-quoted captions, while her spoken and scripted voice is well covered.

## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~13 words; podcast monologue sentences run 10-20 words and stack with 'and' rather than subordinate clauses.
- One-line paragraphs: LinkedIn posts are 1-4 sentences with no line breaks; two of the five posts are a single sentence.
- Hook-type frequency across the sample: maxim-first ~40%, origin-triad anecdote ~25%, named-listener verdict ~20% (podcast Q&A), direct warning or command ~15%.
- Dollar figures: one or two per story, always her own numbers ($1,000 loan, $50,000 Merrill offer, $20 million, $66 million, five billion); no percentages, no cited studies.
- Emoji: zero in the five LinkedIn posts captured. Hashtags: zero.
- Caps for emphasis: reserved for warnings ('do NOT', 'DO NOT'); none elsewhere.
- Exclamation marks: roughly one per LinkedIn post, usually on the revenge or triumph beat ('I would rather die than let him see me not succeed!').
- Profanity: none; the closest is 'son of a gun'. Hedges ('maybe', 'I think'): rare, one in 33 items ('I think business plans are overrated'), and even that one lands as an absolute.
- Absolutes and repetition: 'always', 'never', 'no exceptions', 'I'm repeating that' appear in roughly a third of the podcast items.
- Idiom register: 'man alive', 'boy was I floating', 'forget about it', 'get-go', 'gonna', 'pity party', 'the other guy', 'mom-and-pop', 'trust me on this'.
- Favorite openers: a plain maxim ('An insult is advice in reverse'); a direct address to a named caller ('Jack, prove them wrong'); 'Hey there business owners'.
- Favorite closers: a one-line imperative ('Make it change for yourself.' / 'Don't expect them to change.' / 'Trust me on this.') or the dollar-figure punchline of the story.
- Spoken vs written: the scripted podcast monologues read like written copy and are the best proxy for her long-form written voice; the LinkedIn posts are shorter, plainer, and about half promo for the podcast.

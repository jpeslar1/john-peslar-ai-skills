# Jason Fried Voice Corpus

Research date: 2026-10-18

The excerpts below are short quotations from public posts, essays, a free online book, and one talk, included under fair use for style analysis and attribution. They are third-party content and NOT covered by this repository's MIT license.

**Provenance note.** Items 1-29 are verbatim: copied character for character from pages bylined to Jason Fried personally (HEY World posts signed "-Jason", two Signal v. Noise posts). Items 30-32 are verbatim from the free online edition of Getting Real (37signals, 2006), which carries a collective "by Basecamp" byline; Fried led the book but is not individually credited on those pages, so treat them as the house voice he set. Items 33-35 are DOCUMENTED: YouTube auto-captions (ASR) of his 2010 TEDxMidwest talk, with stutter repeats dropped and one ASR error corrected and flagged; the source has no punctuation. Nothing here comes from a quote aggregator. His X account (@jasonfried) was not sampled because it requires login to fetch.

**Punctuation note.** Items 11 and 26 preserve an em dash because they are verbatim quotations. The operator's copy rule still bans em and en dashes in anything generated in this voice; see the Punctuation Override in SKILL.md.

## Verbatim excerpts, collective byline (Getting Real)

30. "Conventional wisdom says that to beat your competitors you need to one-up them. If they have four features, you need five (or 15, or 25). [...] So what to do then? The answer is less. Do less than your competitors to beat them. Solve the simple problems and leave the hairy, difficult, nasty problems to everyone else. Instead of one-upping, try one-downing. Instead of outdoing, try underdoing."

## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~10 words in HEY posts; fragments of 2-4 words are common; Signal v. Noise essays run ~16.
- One-line paragraphs: roughly half of HEY post paragraphs; the rest are 2-3 sentences.
- Hook-type frequency across the 14 HEY posts sampled: settled-law thesis ~50%, criticism restated ~15%, unsaid thing ~15%, household regression ~10%, reversed credential ~10%.
- Analogy source: every sampled argument-carrying analogy is a trade, craft, kitchen, instrument, or household object; zero tech-on-tech analogies.
- Profanity: 4 instances across ~12,000 words, all at the emotional peak of a paragraph, none in titles.
- Caps and exclamation marks: zero caps-for-emphasis; one or two exclamation marks across the whole sample.
- Hedges: near zero; the one recurring admission of uncertainty ("Certainty is a mirage") is itself stated as a conclusion.
- Sign-off: "-Jason" on every HEY post; no greeting line.
- Favorite openers: a complete declarative thesis; a restated criticism; a dated personal scene (parents' TV, a friend asking what to do with $2M).
- Favorite closers: a flipped cliche or coined phrase ("Legacy isn't a monument, it's just that moment on repeat.") or a 2-4 word imperative ("Go do.").
- Spoken register (ASR sample): same thesis-and-analogy structure, more "like," "you know," "right," heavier second-person "you."

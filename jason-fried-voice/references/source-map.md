## What the corpus rests on

Twenty-nine of thirty-five excerpts are verbatim from pages bylined to Fried personally: fourteen HEY World posts from 2025-2026 (dated via the Atom feed) and two Signal v. Noise essays from 2016-2017. Three are verbatim from the free Getting Real book, which carries a collective byline. Three are documented-grade ASR captions of his TEDx talk. The sample is current (two-thirds of it from the last 20 months), consistent across 20 years of positions, and drawn entirely from his own channels. Confidence in wording is high for items 1-32 and moderate for 33-35.

## Known gaps

- **X was not sampled.** @jasonfried is widely cited as his best short-form corpus, but fetchers are blocked without login. A human can export or screenshot recent posts. Not required for viability since the HEY posts are themselves short-form.
- **The books were not quoted.** Rework, Remote, and It Doesn't Have to Be Crazy at Work have no free primary excerpt that was located. Famous lines ("Meetings are toxic," "Planning is guessing," "Workaholics aren't heroes") exist in the books and may exist on Signal v. Noise, but they were not fetched here and must not be quoted as verbatim from memory.
- **Getting Real is collectively bylined.** Items 30-32 reflect the house voice Fried led rather than text provably typed by him alone.
- **TED's official transcript was not retrievable** (JS-rendered). TEDx excerpts rest on YouTube auto-captions; one ASR error ("quein art" for "Cuisinart") was corrected and flagged.
- **Only 14 of ~30 HEY posts were fetched.** The rest ("Great design was a Cuisinart", "$5300 in $100s", "A beta is like inviting guests over", "Introducing Fizzy") are public at the same URL pattern.
- **Anything published after 2026-08 on HEY, or after 2026-10-18 anywhere, is unsampled.**

## Pattern notes

### 1. Thesis then fragments
A complete declarative sentence, then two or three fragments. The Ghostwriter playbook makes the complete-sentence thesis mandatory so the fragments have something to extend.

### 2. Trade and household analogies
Bladesmith, guitar, restaurant kitchen, Cuisinart, peanut butter on a plate, money that rests. Never a tech analogy for tech.

### 3. Flipped cliche as closer
"Years of evidence," "one-downing," "you boot them up," "Legacy isn't a monument." The skill uses this as the default title and closer move.

### 4. Less as the strategy
Build less, meet less, grow less. Whenever the question is how to beat a competitor, the answer is what to remove.

### 5. Profit as shield, independence as obligation
Profit framed as freedom from games; independence framed as a duty to try the weird idea. These are the bootstrapping arguments in the corpus.

### 6. Say the unsaid
"Most people don't like computers. Nobody in tech wants to say that out loud." Contrarian takes open by naming what the feed hides, then explaining who is doing the talking.

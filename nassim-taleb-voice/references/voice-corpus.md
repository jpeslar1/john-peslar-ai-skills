**Provenance note.** Items 1-19 and 23-28 are verbatim: written by Taleb and published on his own channels (the INCERTO publication on Medium, read in full text via its open RSS feed because Medium's HTML returns 403 to fetchers; and fooledbyrandomness.com, where every notebook quote was grep-verified against the raw 289 KB page). Nothing here comes from a quote aggregator. His X account (@nntaleb) and The Bed of Procrustes were not sampled (see source-map.md); do not quote either from memory. Update 2026-10-19: items 101-110 add an English spoken sample, documented-grade diarized ASR of one EconTalk interview (2022), in the section "Spoken excerpts" below.

**Punctuation note.** Taleb types dashes glued to the following word and uses ampersands in aphorisms; where an excerpt preserves one it is verbatim. The operator's copy rule still bans em and en dashes in anything generated in this voice; see the Punctuation Override in SKILL.md.

## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~22 words in essays, with Latinate sentences reaching 40; notebook aphorisms run 12-25 words in one or two clauses.
- One-line paragraphs: common in essays as the blunt verdict after a formal sentence ("Bureaucrats do not like simple solutions.").
- Hook-type frequency across the sampled essays and entries: inversion ~30%, quoted idiocy then demolition ~25%, asymmetry aphorism ~25%, extend-the-absurdity ~10%, side-effect reframe ~10%.
- Class insults: roughly one per 250 words in polemics; zero named private individuals in the sampled text.
- Hedges: zero. Apologies: zero.
- Foreign interjections and classical anchors: 1-3 per essay (primo/secundo, chi va piano va sano; Livy, Cicero, Santayana, Popper).
- Numbers: small checkable demonstrations in polemics (R0, p squared, 93.75%); none in aphorisms.
- Scare quotes: on the opponent's phrase in nearly every polemic ("evidence based", "old person problem").
- Typos left in: "exceprts", "balloney"; he states he refuses editing.
- Favorite openers: "No, no; it's the exact opposite."; a quoted phrase followed by its demolition.
- Favorite closers: a terse imperative ("Wear a mask. For the Sake of Others."); a demotion ("Just a side effect, you fools.").
- Spoken vs written (items 101-110, one 40-minute EconTalk interview, 2022): the written gears (12-25 word aphorism, 20-35 word Latinate sentence) give way to long run-on turns of 40-80 words held together by 'okay?', 'you see?', and 'you know' check-ins, with stuttered articles ('the, the, the') and restarts ('much, much more'). The written zero-hedge rule does not hold in speech ('I mean', 'I guess', item 102). Calculations become parables (the rabbi and the stone, roommates, the passport) and the aphorism survives only as a short tag at the end of a turn ('It's disproportionate.', 'That's the problem.'). Turns still open on a correction ('No, no, no.') and he self-mocks before he attacks ('You need to have more enemies'). No class insults and no profanity in the sample, which reflects a friendly host; his combative spoken register (Bloomberg, X Spaces) is unsampled.

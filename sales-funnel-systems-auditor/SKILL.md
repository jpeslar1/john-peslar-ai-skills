# Sales Funnel Systems Auditor

Use this skill to diagnose a funnel as an operating system, not a pile of tactics. Focus on the path from attention to trust to conversion to delivery to referrals.

## Core Principle

Most founder-led funnels do not have a traffic problem first. They have a sales-system problem:

- The offer ladder is unclear.
- Too many CTAs compete for the same buyer.
- Nurture does not route by buyer state.
- Pipeline truth is not visible.
- Sales activity depends on the owner's memory, mood, and spare time.
- Proof from delivery is not packaged back into acquisition.

The goal is to turn the funnel into a visible, measurable, repeatable system.

## Audit Workflow

1. **Map the funnel**
   - Top-of-funnel channels
   - Lead capture assets
   - Owned audience
   - Trust-building surfaces
   - Conversion events
   - Offers
   - Delivery experience
   - Upsells, referrals, reactivation

2. **Identify buyer states**
   - Curious learner
   - Problem-aware lead
   - Ready-to-build operator
   - Ready-to-outsource buyer
   - Existing customer
   - Referral source

3. **Score the funnel**
   Use a 1-10 score across:
   - Target market clarity
   - Message clarity
   - Media/channel fit
   - Lead capture
   - Nurture and segmentation
   - Conversion mechanism
   - Delivery experience
   - Lifetime value path
   - Referral system
   - Pipeline visibility

4. **Find the leaks**
   Look especially for:
   - CTA overload
   - Traffic sent to the wrong offer
   - Lead magnets that do not imply the paid offer
   - Newsletter/community nurture with no clear ascension path
   - Webinars with weak follow-up
   - Launches without opens/clicks/replies/deposits tracked
   - Offers that mix DIY, DWY, and DFY without routing logic
   - Fake-full pipeline, stale leads, or no qualification criteria

5. **Design the routing matrix**
   Every warm lead should have one obvious next step based on current state.

6. **Recommend the operating cadence**
   Include weekly review, dashboard metrics, follow-up rules, proof capture, and referral prompts.

## Barron-Style Sales System Lens

Use this diagnostic when the user has founder-led sales, a service offer, a bootcamp, a coaching offer, or a DFY/DWY implementation offer.

The repeated pattern:

- The enemy is not low motivation or bad tools. The enemy is a sales process living in the owner's head.
- A clean pipeline beats a fake-full pipeline.
- More leads into a broken sales system just leaks faster.
- The buyer is buying the bridge from painful current reality to desired future reality.
- A simple sales system has four jobs:
  1. Find someone with a problem you can solve.
  2. Help them see you can solve it.
  3. Make the ROI concrete.
  4. Ask for the business.
- The owner must build the system, make it measurable, install the operator/team layer, and keep improving the bottleneck.

Do not overcomplicate this into a 20-tool stack unless the current stage requires it.

## Offer Ladder Rules

When a business has multiple products, communities, cohorts, services, and software offers, simplify the ladder:

| Buyer state | Best offer type |
|---|---|
| Wants to learn | Free content, newsletter, free community |
| Wants roadmap and unblocking | Paid community or membership |
| Wants one specific outcome built with them | Bootcamp, cohort, DWY implementation |
| Wants outcome without touching the work | DFY managed service |
| Already has the workflow and needs tooling | SaaS product |
| Bought and won | Upsell, testimonial, referral |

Do not pitch every offer at once. Pick one primary CTA per campaign.

## Drip Sequence Rules

For bootcamp, cohort, webinar, or managed-offer campaigns:

1. Start with diagnosis before pitching.
2. Name the painful current state.
3. Show the cost of staying stuck.
4. Prove the mechanism.
5. Reveal the offer as the implementation container.
6. Split Build With Us vs We Run It when both DWY and DFY exist.
7. Use proof before urgency.
8. Track replies and clicks as pipeline stages.
9. Close with a binary future, not generic scarcity.

Useful drip structure:

| Email | Beat |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Pitch warning + diagnostic hook |
| 2 | The real bottleneck |
| 3 | Pipeline truth / hidden leak |
| 4 | Current reality to future reality |
| 5 | Proof or case study |
| 6 | Offer reveal |
| 7 | Value stack and routing options |
| 8 | Objection handling |
| 9 | Guarantee / proof / urgency |
| 10 | Final binary close |

## Rules

- Lead with the leak, not a generic marketing summary.
- Separate traffic problems from conversion-system problems.
- Give one primary money move.
- Make CTAs state-based.
- Include proof capture and referral systems.
- Prefer simple dashboards before complex CRM automation.
- Do not recommend more content, more ads, or more lead magnets until the sales path can handle them.

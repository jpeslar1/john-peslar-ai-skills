# Rand Fishkin Voice Corpus

Research date: 2026-10-19

The excerpts below are short quotations from public posts, articles, and talks, included under fair use for style analysis and attribution. They are third-party content and NOT covered by this repository's MIT license.

**Provenance note.** Items 1-26 are verbatim. Items 1-17 are from his bylined SparkToro blog posts (2025-2026), pulled through the site's WordPress REST API with full post bodies; items 8, 9, and 14 are spoken 5-Minute Whiteboard monologues whose transcripts he pasted into the post himself, so they are treated as verbatim-as-published with his stutters preserved. Items 18-21 are from his 2023 essay The Final Chapter of My First Startup on sparktoro.com, the unpublished epilogue to Lost and Founder. Items 22-26 are his own LinkedIn posts (October 2026), read from logged-out permalinks with the full post text and a machine-readable publish date. Items 27-29 are DOCUMENTED: a Speechpad transcription of a 2015 Moz Whiteboard Friday video, published by Moz under its own Video transcription heading; the content is his but the wording is a third-party transcription. Typographic apostrophes and quotation marks were normalized to straight ASCII; wording is otherwise untouched. Nothing here comes from a quote aggregator. Not sampled: his X account (the syndication timeline was rate-limited and no status IDs were harvested) and the book Lost and Founder itself.

**Punctuation note.** Items 7 and 10 preserve em dashes because they are verbatim quotations. The operator's copy rule still bans em and en dashes in anything generated in this voice; see the Punctuation Override in SKILL.md.

## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~17 words in blog essays, with the longest evidentiary sentences running past 40 words before a 1-3 word slam ("Period.", "Nope."); LinkedIn posts run ~14 words per sentence.
- One-line paragraphs: roughly 20% of essay paragraphs; in the memoir essay the share rises to about half ("$371,000.", "Never in writing. Never on video.").
- Hook-type frequency across the 26 written items: number or provable claim then verdict ~35%, recurring-fad complaint ("It's a day ending in Y", BOALIR filing) ~20%, direct address to "marketing friends" with a correction ~15%, confession or bare figure ~15%, quoted bad advice then rebuttal ~15%.
- Sources named in the sentence: Similarweb, Datos, HubSpot, MIT, QJE, Ashley Liddell; nearly every number arrives with its origin, and guesses are labeled ("my personal educated guess is between 5-10%").
- Caps for a shouted clause: 1 in about 4 LinkedIn posts ("ONLY BECAUSE 'IT MIGHT INFLUENCE AI?!'", "IT'S FRICKIN' FREE PEOPLE!", "THE data"); rare in essays.
- Exclamation marks: 1-3 per LinkedIn post, near zero in essay prose outside quoted hype.
- Emoji: present in about 1 in 4 LinkedIn posts, at the punchline or sign-off (🆓🆓🆓, 🤗, 😉); zero in the blog essays sampled; hashtags: zero.
- Profanity: none beyond "damn sure", "sure as heck", "frickin'", "sucked lemons", "slagged off"; moral words ("evil", "theft", "unrepentant") do the heavy lifting instead.
- Hedges: explicit and quantified ("might be way too high", "Maybe this is a silly complaint", "(most) B2B software companies"); he disclaims his own stake ("this is hardly my fight") before the harshest paragraph.
- Numbers: exact and often uncomfortable; $371,000, 68%, <0.5%, 1/5th and 4/5ths of Americans, 60% and 59%, 17 years, 120 years, 10X.
- Favorite openers: a sourced statistic; "It's a day ending in Y"; "Oh, we need to have a little talk marketing friends"; "I've told this story only once before"; a quoted piece of consensus advice.
- Favorite closers: a warm sign-off ("Good luck, friends. 🤗", "Take care.") and a p.s. crediting someone; in essays, a one-line restatement of the verdict ("Traffic is a vanity metric.").
- Spoken vs written: spoken whiteboard transcripts keep the stutters ("Friends, I I don't know") and "Yes. Sure." tags; the 2015 Moz register ("Howdy, Moz fans") is friendlier and more teacherly than the 2025-2026 indignation; both are sampled, the 2015 one as documented.

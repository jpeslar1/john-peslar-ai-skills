# David Ogilvy Source Map

Research date: 2026-09-09 (book and letter prose added 2026-10-19)
Refresh cadence: every 12 months (the corpus is historical and will not drift; next: 2027-09)

## Corpus quality warning

This was the weakest-sourced corpus in the voice-dna collection. Items 1-38 are `documented`, meaning widely republished and consistently attributed, or in the case of the 2026-09-09 items, either the agency's own official curation or a swipe-file reproduction of an actual advertisement. Items 101-130 (2026-10-19) are `verbatim`: running prose copied from the publisher's own preview of The Unpublished David Ogilvy and from a scan of his 1972 agency advertisement. The two canonical books themselves are still not quoted (see the rights checks below). None of it is `verified` in this project's sense, because Ogilvy died in 1999 and has no site of his own. Ogilvy is one of the most misquoted figures in advertising, and quote sites propagate errors between each other. His own memo says "Check your quotations." Do that before any of these lines appears in client-facing work.

## What was actually fetched

**2026-09-09, original build:** Nothing primary. The corpus was assembled from search results and one quotation aggregator. Two attempts to have a page-reading tool reproduce the memo verbatim were declined by that tool on copyright grounds, which is why the memo items were drawn from fragments that appeared consistently across multiple independent reproductions.

## Rights checks and book-text fetches, 2026-10-19

- **Confessions of an Advertising Man (1963), renewal status.** The Stanford Copyright Renewal Database (https://exhibits.stanford.edu/copyrightrenewals) returned HTTP 403 behind a Cloudflare challenge to both curl and the page-reading tool, so it could not be searched. The US Copyright Office public records API (https://api.publicrecords.copyright.gov, title search "confessions of an advertising man") returned the renewal itself: RE0000541197, registered 1991-08-13, "Confessions of an advertising man. By David Ogilvy.", claimant "David Ogilvy (A)", renewal for A651005 dated 1963-09-27. The book is therefore in US copyright (to the end of 2058). No public-domain route exists.
- **HathiTrust.** The catalog search UI (catalog.hathitrust.org and babel.hathitrust.org full-text search) returned HTTP 403 / Cloudflare challenge. The HathiTrust Bibliographic API did respond and returned no records at all for the 1963 Atheneum edition (LCCN 63017855, OCLC 244600) or for OCLC 1025163, 2547438, 16922248, 30329373, 53457393, LCCN 79025505, 88007605, ISBN 0689706014, 0345021843, 0689102151. No full-view item exists in any identifier checked; given the renewal, none would be expected.
- **Internet Archive.** Every Ogilvy book item found (`confessionsofadv0000davi` 1963 Atheneum, `confessionsofadv0000ogil_r2a4` 1988, `bloodbrainsbeera00ogil`, `autobiography00ogil`) carries `access-restricted-item: true` and sits in `printdisabled` / `inlibrary`: lending or print-disabled access only, not public domain. Not used.
- **Google Books.** The public volumes feed reports `view_no_pages` for every Ogilvy on Advertising, Confessions, and Unpublished David Ogilvy edition listed. No preview to read.
- **Publisher excerpts.** Penguin Random House's Ogilvy on Advertising page (Vintage, 9780394729039) has no excerpt. Oldcastle Books (current UK publisher of Confessions) returned no Ogilvy listing in site search. Profile Books' The Unpublished David Ogilvy page links a free preview PDF; that was used.
- **The 1972 advertisement.** The Copyright Office online catalog returned no record for the title (its online coverage starts in 1978); pre-1978 status was not determined. Quoted in short excerpts for style analysis, as with the other ad copy in this corpus.

## Known gaps

- **The two canonical books are still not quoted, but running prose is now covered.** As of 2026-10-19 the corpus holds 30 `verbatim` items of his own continuous prose: his 1935 Aga sales manual and 1936 advertising talk (from the publisher's preview of The Unpublished David Ogilvy, pp. 3-12) and his 1972 agency advertisement "How to create advertising that sells" (11 of its 38 rules plus the opening and close). What remains: nothing from Confessions of an Advertising Man (in copyright, renewed 1991, no lawful text online) or Ogilvy on Advertising (in copyright, no lawful excerpt found), nothing from The Unpublished David Ogilvy beyond chapter 1 (no memos, letters, or speeches from chapters 2-7), and nothing from his 1950s-1980s middle-career prose. Book lines in items 1-24 are still `documented` only.
- **Hathaway body copy is the weakest-sourced group in this revision.** It was assembled via search synthesis across secondary reproductions, not fetched by this session from one archival page. Confirm against a photographed scan before treating it as exact.
- **No video or spoken register.** YouTube transcript access was blocked this session by an IP-level 429 (see above). This is a new, concrete finding this revision (previously the corpus simply had no video route attempted); the gap itself is not closed.
- **Wording variance.** Several items, particularly "If it doesn't sell, it isn't creative", appear in slightly different forms across sources. The corpus records the most common form and flags the variance. This revision surfaced one more: the Rolls-Royce "At 60 miles an hour" ad is dated 1958 by swiped.co and 1959 by ogilvy.com's own campaign history. Both are cited; neither is silently preferred.
- **Attribution risk.** At least one widely circulated Ogilvy quotation collection includes lines he did not write. Anything not traced to a book, a memo reproduction, a dated advertisement, or the agency's own official reissue should be treated as unattributed.
- **Only two ad campaigns given real depth.** Rolls-Royce and Hathaway got focused research this revision. Schweppes, Dove, Puerto Rico, and Britain tourism are mentioned in the corpus at the level of a single background fact each, not sampled as copy. A refresh could pull actual headlines for each from the swipe-file archive the same way this revision did for Rolls-Royce.

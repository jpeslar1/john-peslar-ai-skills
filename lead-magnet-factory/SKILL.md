# Lead Magnet Factory

Use this skill to turn an offer or audience problem into a lead magnet people actually want. The lead magnet should solve a narrow, urgent problem and naturally lead to the paid offer.

Read `references/templates.md` when the user wants a full outline, DM copy, or landing page copy.

## Core Principle

A strong lead magnet gives away a specific shortcut, not a vague education asset. It should create immediate progress and make the next step obvious.

## Lead Magnet Types

Choose the format based on the audience and offer:

- **Checklist** - Best for operational tasks and repeatable QA.
- **Prompt pack** - Best for AI workflows and execution shortcuts.
- **Template** - Best when users need a reusable artifact.
- **Scorecard** - Best when users do not know where they stand.
- **Swipe file** - Best for marketers, sales teams, and creators.
- **Teardown** - Best when the audience wants expert judgment.
- **Playbook** - Best for multi-step business workflows.

## Creation Workflow

1. Define the paid offer and desired next step.
2. Name the audience's urgent problem.
3. Choose a lead magnet format that creates a fast win.
4. Write 5 angles and select the one with the strongest pull.
5. Build the outline.
6. Add proof, examples, and copy-paste assets.
7. Write the CTA and follow-up sequence.

## Scoring

Score each idea 1 to 10:

- Urgency: does the audience care now?
- Specificity: is the promise concrete?
- Speed: can they get value in minutes?
- Relevance: does it lead to the paid offer?
- Shareability: would someone tag a friend or comment?
- Production ease: can it be built quickly?

Pick the highest total unless it is hard to produce or disconnected from the offer.

## Rules

- Make the lead magnet useful even if the person never buys.
- Avoid giant generic guides.
- The title should promise a concrete result or reveal a painful mistake.
- The CTA should be direct and connected to the offer.
- Do not overcomplicate delivery. A simple doc or hosted page is often enough.

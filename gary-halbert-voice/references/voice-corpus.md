## Verbatim excerpts

### From the thegaryhalbertletter.com homepage letter (public site copy, fetched 2026-09-09)

This page is itself a Halbert sales letter and is the cleanest single sample of the voice available without touching the archive.

1. "From: / Gary C. Halbert / North of Jewfish Creek / Thursday, 8:37 a.m."
   The dateline. Place and time, no year, framing the whole page as a personal letter.

2. "Dear Friend,"
   The salutation. Never skipped.

3. "I have no idea how you got to this website."
   The opening line. A confession that disarms.

4. "Maybe you read about it in some publication. Maybe a friend referred you to it. Perhaps you stumbled onto it by pure dumb luck."
   The stacked speculation, three beats.

5. "But, however you got here, you have arrived at what is, without question, the most valuable website on the entire Internet! / Here's why:"
   The outrageous claim, followed immediately by the reason-why. This is the single most copyable structural move in the voice.

6. "The subscription price was $195.00 per year and, with very little promotion, I soon had readers in more than 50 countries. Then, I started offering a Lifetime Subscription for $2,855.00."
   Exact money, including cents, including the odd number.

7. "And, guess what? It wasn't long until nearly all my subscribers were Lifetime Subscribers!"
   Direct address plus exclamation as rhythm.

8. "So, I got to thinking, "Well, hell... if what I write helps people this much, it should be available to everyone... even if... they can't afford to subscribe to my newsletter.""
   Ellipses as breath marks inside a sentence, and self-quotation as a narrative device.

9. "then... you'll be taken to a page where you'll see a forest of envelope icons"
   Concrete visual language for a mundane instruction.

10. "just click on the envelope it's in... and... shazam! ... the entire text of that issue will appear instantly"
    The playful interjection mid-instruction.

11. "You Will NEVER See / A Scum-Sucking Pop-Up Ad / Or / A Scum-Sucking Banner Ad! / Please excuse my "French"... but... I hate those damn things."
    The centred capitalised callout, then the shared enemy named, then the mild profanity and the wink.

12. "P.S. Don't even think about reproducing and/or selling... even one sentence... of these letters in any form whatsoever."
    The P.S. carrying the strongest instruction in the letter. Also the rights notice this corpus obeys.

13. "Sincerely / Gary C. Halbert / "The Prince Of Print""
    The sign-off with the self-given nickname.

### Widely republished lines from The Boron Letters

These appear in many secondary summaries and reviews. They are labeled `documented`. Verify against the book before reproducing any of them as a direct quotation, and do not reproduce more than a line.

14. The hamburger-stand setup: given every advantage over him in selling burgers, the one advantage he would take instead is "A STARVING CROWD". `documented`

15. The lesson he draws from it: constantly look for groups of people who have already demonstrated that they are hungry for a particular product or service. `documented`

16. The commonly quoted restatement: it is easier to sell a below-average hamburger to a starving crowd than the best hamburger in the world to a well-fed crowd. `documented`, and often paraphrased. Treat as the idea, not as his exact sentence.

## Structural notes for the writer

- The furniture is non-negotiable: dateline, Dear Friend, body, Sincerely, P.S.
- Every large claim is followed within one line by "Here's why" or an equivalent reason-why.
- Money is exact and often ends in an odd figure. $195.00 and $2,855.00, never "about two hundred".
- Ellipses appear inside sentences as pacing, not at the end as trailing off.
- Capitalised centred callouts do the shouting so the body prose can stay conversational.
- Profanity is mild, apologised for with a wink, and used to build alliance against a shared enemy.
- The P.S. is where the sharpest line lives. On this page, it is a threat.

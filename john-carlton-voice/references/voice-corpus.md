# John Carlton Voice Corpus

Research date: 2026-10-19

The excerpts below are short quotations from public posts, articles, and talks, included under fair use for style analysis and attribution. They are third-party content and NOT covered by this repository's MIT license.

**Provenance note.** Items 1-27 are verbatim from John Carlton's own blog, The Rant at john-carlton.com, pulled as full post bodies through the site's WordPress REST API (499 posts, Dec 2004 to Sep 2025, all retrieved; 15 posts quoted, spanning 2005 to 2025). Items 28-32 are documented-grade: a two-part Copyblogger interview transcript (Robert Bruce, Feb-Mar 2012) that the publisher labels "lightly edited for clarity and grammar", so wording is close but not character-exact; use those items for his spoken register, the one-legged-golfer doctrine and Operation MoneySuck, not for exact quotation. marketingrebel.com sales pages were read for context only; they are written in the third person and are not quoted as his voice. Nothing here comes from a quote aggregator. Not sampled: his X accounts (syndication endpoint returned rate-limit stubs), the paid books Kick-Ass Copywriting Secrets of a Marketing Rebel and The Entrepreneur's Guide To Getting Your Shit Together, the 2000s print edition of The Rant, his actual sales letters (the one-legged golfer ad, the Rodale letter), and podcast appearances. His client copy is therefore NOT in this corpus and must not be reproduced as if quoted.

**Punctuation note.** Items 2, 4, 17 and 25 preserve spaced em dashes ("waltz in and — armed with your knowledge of what the market truly wants — offer something") because they are verbatim quotations; his primary pacing device is the ellipsis, including the sentence split across two paragraphs by an ellipsis (items 6, 19, 22, 24, 26), which is preserved here as " / ". The operator's copy rule still bans em and en dashes in anything generated in this voice; see the Punctuation Override in SKILL.md.

## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~14 words in the blog items, with a wide spread: fragments of 2-5 words ("It's a tool.", "Naw.", "… is a loon.") beside 40-word bar-stool runs held together by ellipses and commas; ~24 words in the interview items, which run on and repeat "so".
- One-line paragraphs: roughly 45% of the blog paragraphs sampled are a single sentence or fragment; the signature is a sentence split across two paragraphs by an ellipsis so the second half lands alone ("…is successfully being handed off to the 'bots right now… / … is a loon.").
- Hook-type frequency across the 32 items: rule stated as hard-won doctrine (~30%: "You gotta dig to find it", "It's not the product, it's the ad", "Everyone is scared"), told anecdote then one-line extraction (~25%: the con man, Halbert, quitting smoking), contrarian jab at the crowd or the suits (~20%), self-mocking confession (~15%: "slumbering ape", "worst client I have ever had"), direct-address pep talk (~10%: "Stop being a wuss").
- Ellipses: present in 14 of 32 items, used as breath marks mid-sentence and as the cliffhanger break between paragraphs; this is the dominant punctuation habit.
- Caps for stress: 3 items ("You WANT to find markets", "The client doesn't KNOW"), always on a single word, never a whole phrase.
- Profanity and hard-boiled slang: 7 of 32 items ("bastard", "shit will hit the fan", "complicate the shit out of", "wuss", "loon", "damn the torpedoes", "ain't"); plus coinages "Operation MoneySuck", "moolah", "biz", "Big Dog writers", "wannabe's", "Weak-Ass John vs. Kick-Ass John".
- Numbers: sparse and round, used for credibility rather than proof ("almost 20 years", "30+ years", "400-Yard Drives", "3-card monte"); no prices or response rates in the sampled text.
- Emoji, hashtags: zero. Exclamation marks: 1 in 32 items ("John Does His Job… Finally!" is a post title); he gets emphasis from caps, ellipses and one-line paragraphs instead.
- Hedges: almost none; opinions are stated flat ("Everyone is scared.", "The client doesn't KNOW what the hook is."). Softeners are conversational ("Look…", "Anyway,", "Naw.", "I think it's a mistake"), never institutional.
- Lineage and heroes named in about a quarter of the items: Gary Halbert (by name, twice), con men and old-school street salesmen, the tabloid headline writers; enemies are the suits, the sheep instinct and AI-as-partner.
- Favorite openers: a conversational pivot ("Look…", "Here's what's so hard about", "Anyway, in business,"), a told scene ("I first heard about 'the trust game' from a wizened, street-savvy con man"), or a coined rule with a proper-noun name ("Carlton's First Inconvenient Rule of Entrepreneurship"). The research notes record that every Rant post opens with a dateline, a parenthetical epigraph and "Howdy", which the quoted paragraphs sit below.
- Favorite closers: a toast or punch line in one line ("So here's to us…", "Be a sap. It will help your writing.", "And damn the torpedoes.", "It's a tool."), often followed on the blog by a P.S. with an offer.
- Spoken vs written: the Copyblogger items are looser and more run-on (compound sentences, repeated "so", "you know") but carry the same metaphors and the same second-person address; the blog is tighter, more rhythmic and more profane. Only one spoken source exists in this corpus.

# Grant Cardone Voice DNA

Grant Cardone is the volume evangelist of sales: write everyone up, swing the bat, never worry about your closing ratio. His bylined Entrepreneur columns (2017-2018), his own grantcardone.com blog signed "Be great, GC," and his 2025-2026 X posts share one register: the reader is "you" in nearly every sentence, the sentence is under 15 words, the enemy is "average" and "realistic," and every admission of weakness becomes a rule within a line. This is a hot, maximal register that conflicts with careful, qualified outbound; use it deliberately, not by default. Ground every output in `references/voice-corpus.md` (verbatim excerpts) and `references/source-map.md` (sources and refresh notes).

## Voice DNA

### Cadence and rhythm
- Short declarative bursts, often three in a row with the subject repeated: "I write them up. I swing the bat." / "Tell yourself the truth. Ask for the truth. Hear the truth." Sentences rarely exceed 15 words.
- Opens on a direct second-person question or a flat command: "Where do you get your advice from?" / "If you want money, you've got to get on offense."
- Paragraphs are 1-3 sentences and end on an exclamation mark about half the time: "Settling for average is suicide for your dreams!" / "'Big think' changes the world!"
- Rhetorical question answered by himself, fast: "Look, the now is what? It's the past now! Do you get it?"
- Spoken (interview ASR, items 101-110): the same short bursts, but stacked restarts ("Don't, don't, don't buy one unit"), "okay?" and "by the way" tag checks, and one phrase hammered instead of an exclamation mark: "You don't need cash, you need cash flow."
- Native length: a 500-900 word column or blog post built from 6-10 short paragraphs, signed "Be great, GC" on his own site. On X: one or two lines of product-listing specificity (year, model, hours, square feet).

### Hook patterns
1. **The accusing question** - "Where do you get your advice from? Is it from your friend or your mom?" Deploy as the first line of any column or post; the reader is already implicated.
2. **The flat money command** - "If you want money, you've got to get on offense." Deploy as a thesis line; no setup, no definition.
3. **The binary split** - "There are two groups of people on the internet, spectators (those used by it); and those who use it to market and get themselves known." Deploy to sort the reader into the losing group before offering the exit.
4. **The biography proof point** - "My father died when I was 10. My mother was left with the house and a small insurance settlement." Deploy once per piece, stated without softening, then converted into a rule within two sentences.
5. **The confession turned rule** - "Divorce is not a good thing, but it happened and I'm not ashamed. Settling for average is suicide for your dreams!" Deploy to make vulnerability pay off instantly; never let it sit.
6. **The club-or-bank question** - "Are you at the club more often than the bank?" Deploy as a closer or a mid-piece gut check; one concrete either-or.

### Lexicon
- Branded rules: 10X, 10X-ing it, 10X Balls, the 95/5 Rule, write-up, obscurity, Big think, swing the bat, offense vs defense, "Be great, GC" as sign-off.
- Enemy words: average, realistic, conventional wisdom, quitters, soft, spectators, "the masses who fight for leftovers," the Sunday-evening dread.
- Praise words: exceptional, unreasonable (as a compliment), massive, immense, "giant, radical thinking," "the Alexander and Napoleon of your own life."
- Money stance in four words each: "Money is to be used, not stored." "You can't save your way to wealth." "Debt should never be used for school."
- Connective tissue: "Look," "Remember," "You see," "Do you get it?", "I'm not talking about X here. I'm talking about Y."
- X register: lowercase "i," ampersand for "and," a spaced hyphen as a dash, emoji strings (three crying faces in a row on the jet listing), listing specs (2024 Bombardier Global 7500, 190 hours, 700 BTC, 10,000 sq feet).

### Taboo list
- Never hedge. "Maybe," "I think," "it depends," and "in my experience" are nearly absent from the columns and must stay absent.
- Never apologize, and never apologize for pushing: "Don't worry about pushing too much or becoming overly aggressive."
- Never optimize the closing ratio or qualify the lead out: "They can't buy, won't buy, I don't care. I write them up."
- Never ask a question you do not know the answer to: not "Are you ready to buy?" but "Come on inside and let me get you some more information."
- Never treat saving, a budget, or "realistic" goals as virtues; they are the poor-mindset tells.
- Never defer to credentials, studies, or outside experts; proof is his own biography and his own math.
- Never jargon beyond his own branded terms; never profanity in print beyond "balls" and "sucks."
- Never hashtags.

### Punctuation and formatting
- Exclamation marks at paragraph ends, roughly one per paragraph in columns; none on X.
- Repeated sentence frames for emphasis: "Debt should never be used for school. Debt should never be used for cars. Debt should never be used for anything except to increase income."
- Rhetorical question, then his own answer in the next sentence: "Why? Because..."
- Scare quotes on the enemy word: "realistic," "Big think."
- Ellipses to tease the next line in columns ("Let me ask you a question ..."); on X, a spaced hyphen where a dash would go.
- No headers, no bullets in the sampled columns; the list is carried by repetition ("Only use debt to increase income: Debt should never...").

### POV and stance
- Second person, every sentence. "We" appears only to include himself in a shared failing; "I" appears as the proof ("I'm successful because I do offense moves with my money.").
- Volume over precision: swing at everything, write everyone up, the third strike you watched cross the plate is the sin.
- Attention is the scarce asset; obscurity is the enemy, and self-promotion is a duty, not a vice.
- Money plays offense: no saving, no consumer debt, income-producing debt only, 95 percent of effort on making money.
- Unreasonable is a compliment; firm is respected, soft is not: "People don't appreciate you for being soft."
- Biography is proof and never grievance: no silver spoon, father dead at 10, divorce, third strike, all converted into rules.

### Emotional register
- Hot, loud, certain, commanding. The reader is being pushed, dared, and sorted.
- Boastful on purpose; the jet, the beach house, and the 700 BTC are shown, not hidden.
- Vulnerability lasts exactly one sentence and then becomes a command.
- Not the data coach (Chris Orlob), not the scrappy storyteller with the dollar-figure anecdote (Barbara Corcoran), not the zero-pressure detachment of Josh Braun. He is the sales-floor general who wants more swings, not better aim.
- This register conflicts with careful, qualified outbound and with the operator's own brand. The operator's library marks it "Avoid when: default John-brand copy." Use it only when the user asks for it by name, for a product or audience that wants hype, or as a contrast draft; never make it the default for cold outreach, LinkedIn posts under the operator's name, or client-facing copy.
- When a draft reads tentative, delete every hedge and make the sentence a command; when it reads like a lecture, cut the sentence to under 15 words and add the accusing question; when it reads too hot for the audience, that is a signal to switch voices, not to soften this one.

## Signature Moves

1. **Swing the bat** - reframe any hesitation as a strike you watched cross the plate; the sin is not swinging. Deploy in sales motivation and pipeline-volume arguments.
2. **Write everyone up** - refuse to qualify the buyer out; treat the write-up as the close. Deploy in any closing or process conversation.
3. **Offense vs defense** - every money or strategy question answers with "get on offense." Deploy in finance and growth takes.
4. **Triple frame** - three sentences, same frame, escalating: "Tell yourself the truth. Ask for the truth. Hear the truth." Deploy once per piece as the rule block.
5. **Confession to command** - one line of biography or failure, then the rule it proves. Deploy near the top of any long piece.
6. **The sorting binary** - spectators vs users, average vs exceptional, poor mindset vs successful. Deploy to open contrarian takes.
7. **The known-answer question** - replace "Are you ready to buy?" with a question or instruction whose answer is already yes. Deploy in scripts.
8. **Fight obscurity** - name attention as the asset and self-promotion as the job. Deploy in brand and content takes.
9. **The sign-off** - "Be great, GC" on his own blog. Deploy only as a model for the user's own two-word sign-off, never his.

## Playbooks

### Sales Closer
**Use when** the user runs a volume sales motion, a showroom or inbound close, or needs a scripted push that refuses to pre-qualify.
1. Write everyone up. Do not sort buyers into can-buy and can't-buy before the write-up; the write-up is the close.
2. Ask only known-answer questions: not "Are you ready to buy?" but "Come on inside and let me get you some more information."
3. Make the buyer earn commitment from you, not the other way around: "You have to earn that."
4. Name fear as the real objection, yours not theirs: "If you don't write up the deal because you fear rejection, then you are already defeated."
5. Measure swings, not ratio: count write-ups per day; ignore closing percentage.
6. Close with a command and an exclamation mark, then stop.
- Voice rules: sentences under 15 words; second person throughout; no features list; no hedge; one biography proof at most.
```
Write-up rule: [everyone gets written up; what the write-up is]
Known-answer question: [the instruction or question whose answer is yes]
Earn-it reversal: [what the buyer must earn from you]
Fear named: [the seller's fear, called out]
Swing count: [daily write-ups target, a number]
Command close: [one line, exclamation mark]
```

### Objection Handling Expert
**Use when** the user faces "too expensive," "I need to think about it," "we're being realistic," or "you're too aggressive."
1. Sort the objection into a binary and name the losing side: realistic vs big think, defense vs offense, spectator vs user.
2. Reflect the objection as fear, then flip it: "When you are in fear, your fears come true."
3. If price: money is to be used, not stored; the cost is staying average. Do not discount; discounting is defense.
4. If "too aggressive": "Being unreasonable is a compliment." Hold the line.
5. If "we need advice first": ask where the advice comes from; "an average person can't teach you how to be exceptional."
6. End on a dare: "Come fly with me up here."
- Voice rules: one binary per objection; no multi-point rebuttal; the objection is never validated, only sorted.
```
Objection: [their words]
Binary: [losing side] vs [winning side]
Fear flip: [the fear behind it, named and reversed]
Rule: [one money or mindset rule, under 15 words]
Dare: [one-line close]
```

### Contrarian Take Generator
**Use when** the user wants a hot, polarizing money, sales, or mindset take for a product or audience that rewards hype.
1. Pick a consensus virtue (saving, work-life balance, a high closing ratio, humility, "being realistic") and declare it the poor-mindset tell.
2. Open with the accusing question or the flat command. No wind-up.
3. Sort the reader with a binary in sentence two or three.
4. Drop one biography proof point in one sentence, convert it to a rule in the next.
5. Deliver the triple frame as the rule block.
6. Close on an exclamation-mark rule or an either-or gut check ("Are you at the club more often than the bank?").
7. Before shipping, check the audience: if this is for the operator's own brand or a careful B2B buyer, stop and route to a cooler voice in this repo.
- Voice rules: 150-400 words; 6-10 one- to three-sentence paragraphs; no hedge, no citation, no hashtag; one exclamation mark per paragraph maximum.
```
[Accusing question or flat command]
[Binary sort, one sentence]
[Biography proof, one sentence] [Rule it proves, one sentence]
[Triple frame]
[Consensus virtue named as the poor-mindset tell]
[Exclamation rule or either-or gut check]
```

## Anti-patterns

- Hedging. One "maybe" or "I think" and the voice is gone.
- Qualifying the buyer out, optimizing the closing ratio, or pre-sorting leads. The register is volume, not precision.
- Validating the objection before answering it; he sorts it, he does not honor it.
- Citing a study, an expert, or a statistic that is not his own math. Proof is biography and arithmetic.
- Letting a confession breathe for more than one sentence; it must become a rule immediately.
- Softening the boast. If the jet is in the story, the jet is shown.
- Using this register as the operator's default. It conflicts with careful, qualified outbound and with the operator's own brand; the library says "Avoid when: default John-brand copy." A cold email, LinkedIn post, or client deliverable that drifts into this voice without being asked for is a defect.
- Over-imitation tells: "10X" in every paragraph; an exclamation mark on every sentence; the triple frame used three times in one piece; "Look," opening every paragraph; a biography proof in every section.
- Presenting his stories (father dead at 10, the house sold within a month, the divorce, the third strike, the cable-network board, the jet and the 700 BTC house) or his branded rules (10X, 95/5, write everyone up) as the user's own. They are his. Credit or rename them.
- Writing him as spoken-stage Cardone. The only spoken sample is one interview (items 101-110); the stage and keynote cadence is still inferred from written passages that mimic speech, not from a transcript.

## Punctuation Override

Cardone uses an em dash occasionally in long-form ("10X Balls—whatever gender you are") and a spaced hyphen as a dash on X ("Irreplaceable real estate - i'll regret selling it"). The operator's global copy rule bans em and en dashes in all published output. When writing AS this voice for the operator, convert each one to a period and a new short sentence, which matches his burst rhythm better than the dash did; a colon is acceptable before a rule. Do not reproduce the X-style spaced hyphen as a dash substitute in long-form copy. This is an intentional override of the authentic punctuation, not an error.

- His biography and his branded rules are his. The playbooks borrow the form (accusing question, binary sort, confession to command, triple frame); the content must be the user's own and the branded terms credited.
- This register is a deliberate choice, never a default. Do not apply it to the operator's own outbound, LinkedIn, or client copy unless asked by name; the operator's library marks it "Avoid when: default John-brand copy."

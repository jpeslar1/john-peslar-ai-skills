# Steph Smith Voice Corpus

Research date: 2026-10-19

The excerpts below are short quotations from public posts, articles, and talks, included under fair use for style analysis and attribution. They are third-party content and NOT covered by this repository's MIT license.

**Provenance note.** Items 1-29 are verbatim text Steph Smith wrote herself, copied from four channels she owns: items 1-18 from eighteen essay passages on blog.stephsmith.io (full post bodies pulled from the Ghost RSS feed for the 15 most recent posts, Mar 2019 to Nov 2020, plus four older posts fetched as HTML, Dec 2018 to Feb 2019; 11 of the site's 25 posts are quoted); items 19-26 from her X account @stephsmithio via the fxtwitter single-status JSON endpoint (2020-2026, incl. the generation-defining-stats thread, the Internet Pipes pricing ask, the data-viz thread and the Groq announcement); items 27-29 from the first-person landing copy of her own products, Internet Pipes and Doing Content Right. Items 30-31 are documented-grade: a podscripts.co machine transcript of the ~2-minute monologue she narrates as new host of the a16z Podcast (7 Nov 2022), which garbles her surname as "Stepsman"; use them for cadence and stance, not exact wording. Nothing here comes from a quote aggregator or a secondary summary. Not sampled: her X profile timeline beyond the 15 cached posts (the syndication endpoint returned 429 this session), any LinkedIn post, the paid text of Doing Content Right and Internet Pipes, Trends.co reports, and conversational interview speech (Sydlis, My First Million, a16z interview episodes).

**Punctuation note.** Item 23 preserves a spaced en dash ("I – no exaggeration – have at least 300 resources") and item 12 preserves a small hyphen-minus glyph ("at the fringe﹣something only a few experts have access to﹣") because both are verbatim quotations. Her essays otherwise run on commas, semicolons and colons, and her X posts on line breaks and emoji bullets. The operator's copy rule still bans em and en dashes in anything generated in this voice; see the Punctuation Override in SKILL.md.

"Interestingly enough, I realized that it was not the sporadic highs that were exceptional, but instead the long hauls; the sequences of events that seemed minimal at each juncture, but compounded into major gains.     How to Be Great? Just Be Good, Repeatably, blog.stephsmith.io, 2019-06-12, https://blog.stephsmith.io/how-to-be-great/, verbatim

3. "Many people, in theory, want to be "great". Yet, many people in life realistically do not want to put in the effort over a sustained period of time to actually get to 1. ## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~19 words in the essays (long compound sentences joined by semicolons and "so"), ~11 words on X, ~12 words in the spoken monologue.
- One-line paragraphs: roughly 70% of lines in the X posts are a single sentence or fragment ("Per day!", "People are mostly good.", "That's me."); in the essays under 10%, where the unit is a 3-5 sentence paragraph that lands on a quotable line.
- Hook-type frequency across the 31 items: stat or dated fact first (~30%: Bezos and 2300%, 60m commute hours, 1000 monthly searches, 30+ years of Excel), rhetorical pivot question (~20%), aphoristic compression (~20%: "Writing and thinking are two peas in a powerful pod", "When a skill becomes obsolete, so do you"), personal admission or career narrative (~15%), product pitch with a reason (~15%).
- Numbers habit: a figure with a unit and a source in about half the items (2300% per year, 60m hours per day, 20m subs, 500+ words, 3 offers within weeks, $30, 300 resources, 22 exercises); she rarely asserts without one.
- Hedges: "I think" appears in 7 of 31 items, plus "Perhaps", "I must admit", "I've come to realize", "arguably", "In fact"; the stance is exploratory and the confidence sits in the data, not the tone.
- Rhetorical questions: present in about a third of the items, usually as the turn of a paragraph ("So then the question then becomes, how does one activate this process?") or as a direct ask to the audience ("What are the generation-defining stats of today?").
- Emoji: 9 across the 8 X posts (💡, 📊, 1️⃣2️⃣3️⃣, 🚀, 💫), used as bullets and section markers, never mid-sentence; zero in the essays and product copy.
- Caps for emphasis: zero; exclamation marks: about one per X post ("Per day!", "quite the monster!", "30+ years!") and roughly one per essay; profanity: zero across all 31 items ("suck" is the ceiling).
- Internet-native shorthand on X only: "bc", "inspo", "folks", trademark joke ("Year of Sponge™"); Canadian spelling in the essays ("favour", "behaviour").
- Attribution habit: sources and people credited by name in about a quarter of the items (Jeff Bezos, @sydlis, @GroqInc, r/dataisbeautiful); she frames herself as a sleuth ("Uncovering trends is literally my job"), never as a guru.
- Favorite openers: a concrete number or dated fact followed by a one-line reframe; "Interestingly enough"; "If I were starting out ... today"; "Consider this."
- Favorite closers: an invitation to participate rather than a hard CTA ("Comment with a trend I missed!", "design a process that works for you, and push forward with no intention of an expiry date"), a compressed aphorism, or a forward-looking sign-off ("See you in the future! 🚀").
- Spoken vs written: the one spoken sample is a scripted host monologue and is cleaner and more declarative than the essays, with short cadenced sentences and a triplet device ("From the ability to work from anywhere, to computers that fit your pocket, to the cost of sequencing the entire human genome"); conversational interview speech is not represented.

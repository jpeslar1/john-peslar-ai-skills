## Bottoms-Up Taxonomy

### Personal Operating System

Recurring ideas:

- Do not frame life as a finish line.
- Keep playing the game instead of retiring into status.
- Define success internally, not through possessions or public scorekeeping.
- Use honesty to build relationships, taste, and judgment.
- Delete attention traps and reduce rumination through action and environment design.
- Build stamina and work ethic without needing applause.

### Business Strategy and Technical Leverage

Recurring ideas:

- The best opportunities often appear when a technical discontinuity creates a new bottleneck.
- Back the technical person or mechanism without which the company would not exist.
- Beware org theater when the core question is whether something hard can be built.
- Look for hidden infrastructure constraints, not just visible market narratives.
- Strategy improves when it identifies the actual system bottleneck.

### Investing and Capital Allocation

Recurring ideas:

- Investing is not a get-rich-quick game.
- Compounding is easier to understand when modeled manually.
- Start small and quietly instead of waiting for social validation.
- Study products and companies you genuinely understand and value.
- Losses are inevitable, so learn what went wrong.
- Sizing and backup plans matter because ego and greed break investors.

### Learning and Thesis Building

Recurring ideas:

- Read for pattern recognition, not intellectual decoration.
- Study history, game theory, science, and technical shifts.
- Articles and current research can matter more than books when fields move quickly.
- Build a prepared mind so a non-obvious opportunity is recognizable.
- Convert research into a thesis and then into action.

### Hard-Truth Coaching

Recurring ideas:

- Rumination is often avoidance disguised as thinking.
- Status symbols can be insecurity with better lighting.
- Taste requires honesty.
- Playing someone else's game is a waste of life.
- If the plan needs constant external validation, it probably will not compound.

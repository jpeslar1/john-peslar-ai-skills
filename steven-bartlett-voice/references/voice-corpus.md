# Steven Bartlett Voice Corpus

Research date: 2026-10-18

The excerpts below are short quotations from public posts, articles, and talks, included under fair use for style analysis and attribution. They are third-party content and NOT covered by this repository's MIT license.

**Provenance note.** Items 3-22 are the only material written in his own hand that could be fetched: his X posts (@StevenBartlett), pulled directly from the X syndication timeline with his spelling and typos intact. The 2018-2019 posts are complete; 2023-2025 long posts come back truncated at roughly 280 characters by the syndication endpoint, and every truncation is marked. Items 1-2 are the first-person opening of his book Happy Sexy Millionaire as quoted on the Hachette UK publisher page; the text is his but the access path is the publisher's blurb, not a book copy. Items 23-29 are DOCUMENTED: YouTube auto-captions (ASR) of two solo videos on his own channels (a 2021 Diary Of A CEO Q&A episode and a 2025 Behind The Diary vlog). Items 30-32 are DOCUMENTED: auto-captions of his TEDxLondon talk and a BBC Maestro course clip. Items 33-36 are DOCUMENTED: his words as transcribed by journalists (Forbes Australia, BusinessCloud), not fetched from his own channel. Nothing here comes from a quote aggregator. His Substack has zero public posts, his LinkedIn is behind login, and no publisher extract exists for The Diary Of A CEO: The 33 Laws, so there is no long-form written sample.

**Punctuation note.** No em or en dashes appear in the sampled text; his pause marks are "..." and the occasional "…", and the one "-" in item 21 is a plain hyphen as he typed it. The operator's copy rule bans em and en dashes in anything generated in this voice regardless; see the Punctuation Override in SKILL.md.

## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~16 words in X posts; aphorism posts are two to four sentences totalling 40-60 words; spoken answers run 40-80 words per unpunctuated chain.
- One-line paragraphs: the 2018-2019 posts are two paragraphs split by a blank line (claim, then the softer truth); 2023-2025 posts open with one celebratory line then run on.
- Hook-type frequency across the 20 X excerpts: flat contrarian claim ~35%, "Maybe…" reframe ~10%, confession or state-of-mind opener ~20%, origin-story receipt ~20%, numbered regret or advice list ~15%.
- Ellipses: one "..." per post in about half of the 2018-2019 posts, placed as a breath before the punchline ("eurghhh, nevermind").
- Exclamation marks: zero in aphorism posts; 2-4 in celebratory posts ("WOW!", "born in Gaborone, in Botswana, Africa!").
- Emoji: sparing. 🙏🏾❤️ as a gratitude sign-off, 🚨 as an announcement marker, 😵‍💫😮 for "surreal"; none in the 2018-2019 aphorisms.
- Caps for emphasis: "WOW!" and "ANNOUNCEMENT" only; no mid-sentence caps.
- Profanity: zero in writing; mild and solo on video ("pissed off").
- Hashtags: one, in 2019 (#instagramdown); none after.
- Numbers: biographical and borrowed. Ages and years (18, 25, 32, 2017), a $100 mic, Watson's $600k; percentages appear only in a video title (80%). He does not cite his own metrics in his own voice.
- Hedges: "Maybe" and "might just be" soften a provocation after it lands, never before; "I think" appears in spoken answers.
- Favorite openers: "I'm just going to go ahead and say it..."; "Trust me."; "Maybe…"; "WOW!"; "In 2017, I started a podcast in my bedroom…"; on video, "I'm going to be honest."
- Favorite closers: a two-to-four word imperative ("Focus on that.", "then let someone down."), or a gratitude line to supporters.
- British spelling throughout (fulfilment, modelled, favourite); spoken register adds "stay with me", "guys", named frameworks ("the someday shelf", "the quitting framework") and anecdotes about named guests.

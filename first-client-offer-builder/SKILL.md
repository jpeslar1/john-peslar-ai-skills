# First-Client Offer Builder

Create one offer a real buyer can repeat and the seller can deliver. Optimize for a first paid engagement within the user's acquisition window, usually 90 days. Keep that seller goal separate from the result and timeline promised to the customer.

## Ground the offer

Use the ICP sentence, disqualifiers, conversation evidence, delivery capabilities, available support hours, proof, and existing draft. Prefer three buyer quotes if available. Without buyer evidence, produce a provisional offer and the questions needed to test it. Do not treat a guessed niche as validated.

Identify the buyer's desired outcome, current workaround, trigger, feared effort, and risk. Translate tools into the business job they perform. Choose one outcome and a measurable acceptance condition. Preserve an existing scope the user has chosen unless evidence exposes a delivery problem.

## Diagnose the four levers

Use the equation with explicit grouping:

`Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood) / (Time Delay x Effort & Sacrifice)`

Rate each lever 1-5 with reasons. For dream outcome and likelihood, 5 is stronger. For time delay and effort, 5 is a greater burden and worse. An unknown gets `unknown`, not an invented middle score. These are qualitative diagnoses, not measurable probabilities; do not multiply them into a fake conversion prediction. Price is considered separately from this equation.

Repair the weakest supported lever first. Strengthen likelihood with relevant proof, a demonstration labeled as such, a smaller pilot, or clear acceptance criteria. If the user has no results yet, do not invent a case study. Reduce time to the first useful result and specify the customer's required work.

## Build one stack

Describe each component, the obstacle it removes, delivery method, owner, and boundary:

1. Core deliverable that produces the promised result.
2. Speed aid that shortens the route to first value.
3. Ease aid that reduces customer effort.
4. Support with a clear duration and response scope.
5. Risk reversal with a measurable trigger and capped remedy.

A speed or ease aid should be useful, not an unrelated bonus added to inflate value. Avoid six packages and invented bonus valuations. For automation, name the workflow, included integrations, required data/access, handover, monitoring, and excluded custom work when they affect delivery.

Choose a credible risk reversal: a paid pilot, acceptance-based revision window, or exit clause can be more feasible than promising revenue or meetings. Specify eligibility, measurement, deadline, buyer dependencies, exclusions, remedy, and maximum exposure. Do not propose unlimited free work. Treat commercial terms as a draft until the user approves them.

## Write and test

Use this shape, simplifying it if that improves readability:

`I help [ICP] get [outcome] in [delivery timeframe] without [unwanted effort/risk]. Includes [core plus speed/ease aids] and [support]. If [measurable failure condition], [bounded remedy].`

A first-time provider might offer a scoped working workflow with a measured baseline instead of guaranteeing the buyer a $3k retainer in 30 days. Numerical examples in the deck illustrate phrasing; they are not evidence that the user can promise those results.

Adapt the packaging by lane: a freelancer's bounded first engagement, an agency's pilot before an ongoing service, or an operator's two-week pilot with sponsor, resources, and success metric. Keep retention tied to ongoing value.

Prepare a say-it-back test for three real matching buyers or warm contacts: who is it for, what changes, what must they do, what happens if it fails, and what feels unclear or hard to believe? Record exact reactions. AI role-play may reveal confusing language but cannot substitute for these three people.

## Deliver

- One recommended offer paragraph, plus alternatives only when useful or requested.
- Four-lever scorecard with evidence and the weakest-lever fix.
- Delivery stack, exclusions, dependencies, effort, and draft risk reversal.
- **WEAK / REWRITE / STRONG** assessment: features only; real outcome with unclear terms; or clear, credible, feasible, and repeatable by buyers. Without buyer tests label any STRONG assessment provisional.
- Three-person test script and a reaction log, followed by the next revision decision.

## Working agreement

Use context already supplied. Ask only for missing facts that change the next decision, preferably in one short batch, then make a useful first draft. Label assumptions and distinguish buyer evidence from your interpretation. Never invent interviews, prospect facts, results, testimonials, budget, or meetings. A target date is a planning goal, not a promised commercial outcome. Use plain sentences and normal hyphens in generated copy.

Create drafts and plans within the requested scope. Sending messages, booking meetings, publishing, changing a CRM, or committing the user to commercial terms requires authorization for that action. A request for advice alone does not authorize execution. Work from pasted notes if tools are unavailable; identify what remains unverified.

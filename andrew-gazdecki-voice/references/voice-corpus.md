# Andrew Gazdecki Voice Corpus

Research date: 2026-10-19

The excerpts below are short quotations from public posts, articles, and talks, included under fair use for style analysis and attribution. They are third-party content and NOT covered by this repository's MIT license.

**Provenance note.** All 30 items are verbatim and written by him. Items 1-19 are his own X posts (@agazdecki, 2021 to October 2026), fetched one by one from the syndication single-post endpoint with ISO dates; posts longer than 280 characters were completed from the fxtwitter status API, and the newest is six days old at research time. Items 20-24 are from public LinkedIn post permalinks, which render full text and a JSON-LD publish date logged out. Items 25-30 are from first-person pieces on the Acquire.com blog, pulled through the site's WordPress JSON endpoint under his author id: two 2023 founder letters that open "Andrew here" and two 2019 bootstrapping essays from the Bizness Apps era. Only first-person pieces were used because that author id is also the house byline on dozens of SEO guides, and even the 2023 letters may have had marketing help. Nothing here comes from a quote aggregator. Not sampled: his book Getting Acquired (no public excerpt), any spoken material (podcasts), and the bulk of his high-frequency 2024-2025 X posting (the timeline endpoint rate-limits; items were reached by status ID), so every item is written and several 2026 X items are listing promos rather than opinion.

**Punctuation note.** Item 27 preserves a spaced en dash because it is a verbatim quotation from a 2023 founder letter; his X and LinkedIn posts in this sample use line breaks and full stops instead. The operator's copy rule still bans em and en dashes in anything generated in this voice; see the Punctuation Override in SKILL.md.

## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~10 words on X and LinkedIn, where every sentence is its own line; ~22 words in the 2019 blog essays and 2023 letters.
- One-line paragraphs: roughly 85 percent of X and LinkedIn lines; multi-sentence paragraphs appear only in the blog pieces.
- Hook-type frequency across the 30 excerpts: flat founder truism ~30% ("Startup founders should remember that nobody really knows what they're doing at the beginning."), receipt or metric stack ~25% ("82 acquisitions successfully closed", "$1.1M TTM revenue"), origin-story credential ~15% ("I bootstrapped a SaaS to $10m ARR."), announcement or shout-out ~15% ("Getting Acquired is finally out!", "Shout out to the crazy entrepreneurs"), deadpan joke ~15% ("Dark mode is worth billions.", 9 cakes for Twitter).
- Repeated mantras: "For the love of startups this is why @acquiredotcom exists." appears in 3 of 30 items across two channels; "We're just getting started" in 2; "life changing" or "life-changing" in 4. Reuse these lines rather than inventing new slogans.
- Brand-as-verb: the company name becomes a past participle in 5 of 30 items ("MicroAcquire'd", "@acquiredotcom'd", "acquire.com'd for $100m"); the @handle appears in roughly half of all X items.
- Numbers: in about 2 of 3 items; money in lowercase-m style ("$10m ARR", "$3.5m", "$25m") on X and LinkedIn, hyphenated spans ("8-years", "2-years", "22-year old", "6-figures"), and one percentage (72%, 163%) only inside metric stacks.
- Bullet stacks: leading hyphens or chevrons for receipts in 4 items ("- 82 acquisitions", "> $361K TTM profit"); the two-column outcome comparison (item 22) is the signature argument format.
- Exclamation marks: about 1 per 3 items, always sincere ("Great time to be a startup founder.", "It's a great time to be a founder!", "over $1M in revenue!!"); doubled at most once.
- Profanity: one instance in 30 ("losing your shit"). Snark at named people: zero. Emoji: zero in the sampled text. Hashtags: zero.
- Hedges: rare; "I'm biased but", "somehow achieved", and "dare I say it" are the only softeners, and each sits next to a sincere claim.
- Favorite openers: a flat truism about founders; a metric or revenue figure for a listing ("This startup was doing over $100K a year."); the bootstrapped-to-$10m origin line.
- Favorite closers: the mantra ("For the love of startups this is why @acquiredotcom exists.", "We're just getting started!"), a one-line verdict ("That's the play.", "Both huge successes. Just different."), or a P.S. offer to help sell the reader's startup; the blog letters close "Here's to you and your success."
- Spoken register: unsampled. All 30 items are written; treat any spoken-style output as inferred from the X and letter registers.

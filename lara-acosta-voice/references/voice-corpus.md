## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~9 words; hooks 8-10 words by her own stated rule.
- One-line paragraphs: an estimated 70%+ of post lines; blank line between nearly every unit.
- Hook-type frequency in sampled posts: time-anchored personal beat ~35%, bold-claim gift ~25%, status-jump contrast ~15%, guru takedown ~15%, wordplay flip ~10%.
- Rehooks: parenthetical or contrast line 2 appears in roughly half the sampled posts.
- Emoji: minimal in body text; arrows (→, ↳) do the visual work instead.
- Caps: selective single-word emphasis (SHOULD, FAQ); no full-caps lines.
- Profanity: none observed.
- Typos: present and left in ("its", "comeback", "obssesing"); polish is not the brand.
- Favorite openers: dated personal beats ("10 months ago", "1 week ago", "Tomorrow").
- Favorite closers: 1-2 line directives ("Stop procrastinating your success.") and P.S. lines carrying FAQ jokes or the newsletter CTA.

Email-specific stats (from the 8 verified full emails above):
- Sentence length runs longer in email than on LinkedIn: median ~14 words, with conditional stack sentences reaching 25-35 words; still one sentence (occasionally two) per paragraph, blank line between every unit.
- Greeting: usually none; opens mid-thought or with a direct question. When a greeting exists it is "Hey [name] -" or the name woven into line 1. Reader's first name recurs 2-4 times per email mid-body.
- Sign-off: "Love," or "See you inside," then "Lara x" alone on a line. Never "Best" or "Cheers".
- Ellipsis ("...") is the dominant connector, ending 3-8 lines per email to pull the reader down the page.
- Caps: single pivot words mid-sentence (ZERO, NOT, NOW, REALLY, STILL, OR, AND, WHILE); no full-caps lines.
- Lists: plain hyphen bullets for offer stacks and behavior checklists; "Path 1 / Path 2 / Path 3" choice frames.
- CTA: one bracketed-hyperlink sentence on its own line, repeated at most twice per email; hard deadline or discount scarcity in most sequence emails.
- Emoji: near zero in body; one heart in a subject line.
- P.S.: reply-bait ("Reply to this email and tell me. I read every single one") rather than a link.

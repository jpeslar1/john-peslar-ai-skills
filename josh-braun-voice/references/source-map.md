His public identity: selling is sorting, not convincing ("Sort me, don't sell me"); detach from the outcome; illuminate problems and let prospects persuade themselves.

## Distinct Josh Braun Patterns

### 1. The 4T cold email

Trigger (relevant observation), Think (a question that loosens the grip of the status quo), Third-party validation (a credibility story of someone like the prospect; his gloss cites the bandwagon effect), Talk (low-friction interest check). Verified from his own LinkedIn template post plus secondary articles. Note the Ts are Trigger, Think, Third-party validation, Talk; variants circulating elsewhere ("Teach") are not his.

### 2. Poke the bear

A neutral question that surfaces a risk or cost of inaction the prospect may not see, grounded in loss aversion. Cold-call sequence on his site: Permission, Verification, Context, Problem Identification, Labeling, Poke the Bear, Teaser.

### 3. Illumination questions

Questions that shine a light on a problem or missed opportunity so the prospect persuades themselves: "What are you doing to avoid overpaying property taxes?"

### 4. Detach from the outcome

Neediness repels; attachment creates abandonment. Follow-ups give value or label the silence; the breakup line is the one-sentence "Have you given up on this project?" (credited by Braun to Chris Voss).

### 5. Objections are truths, not obstacles

Defuse ("That's ok."), discover with a menu question, label what you hear. Never argue. For incumbent vendors: poke the bear on the unexamined cost, never attack the vendor.

### 6. Sea of sameness

Jargon-heavy copy is "a vanilla scoop in a sea of vanilla scoops". His recurring phrase is "sea of sameness"; the paraphrase "sameness kills replies" was NOT found verbatim and should not be quoted as his.

### 7. Miniature poetics

One-line paragraphs, everyday analogies (dating, fishing, gardening, ice cream, video games), psychology named casually, and a genuine human beat to close.

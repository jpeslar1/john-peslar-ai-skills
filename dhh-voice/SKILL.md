# DHH (David Heinemeier Hansson) Voice DNA

DHH is the polemicist of the 37signals house: the Rails creator who writes short, hot essays on HEY World that open on a receipt (90% to 1%, $1.5 million a year, half a million dollars of servers), state a thesis as if the argument were already over ("Rails is omakase." / "Paradise is not lost, the world is not ending"), run one metaphor to the final line, and close with a dare or a blessing. He swears, he says "Nay," he uses exclamation marks, and he argues against a strawman quoted in the opponent's own voice. Where Jason Fried lowers the temperature, DHH raises it, with the same conviction underneath. Ground every output in `references/voice-corpus.md` (verbatim excerpts) and `references/source-map.md` (sources and refresh notes).

## Voice DNA

### Cadence and rhythm
- A 25-35 word setup sentence, then a 2-6 word punch: "Technological advancement has always rendered some jobs unnecessary. That is literally the progress!" / "Bzzzzzzzzt!! Wrong!"
- Median sentence around 16 words with a wide spread; paragraphs are 2-5 sentences; the closing paragraph is one or two lines.
- Opens cold on a fact or a thesis, no throat-clearing: "In the 18th century, 90% of Americans worked in agriculture to feed themselves and the nation. Today, it's around 1%."
- Rhetorical questions in about a quarter of paragraphs, answered within a line: "Do you know how many insanely beefy servers you could purchase on a budget of half a million dollars per year?"
- Spoken (Lex Fridman #474, documented ASR): the same verdict-first answers ("It's a disaster.", "100% that's true.") but longer chained sentences, a steelman then "Now, I don't buy that.", and casual profanity ("God damn it").
- Native length: a 300-900 word HEY World post; the long-form essay (omakase, the monolith, the cloud exit) runs 1,500-2,500 words with the same rhythm; X posts are 2-4 sentences in the same voice.

### Hook patterns
1. **The dated receipt** - a number with a century or a year attached in sentence one: "In the 18th century, 90% of Americans worked in agriculture... Today, it's around 1%." Deploy as the first line of any progress, economics, or technology argument.
2. **The settled thesis** - state the conclusion as already decided: "Rails is omakase." / "Paradise is not lost, the world is not ending, and most material metrics of humanity have never been better." Deploy as the opener of a contrarian post, before any evidence.
3. **The scene from my week** - "Two weeks ago at Rails World, I told my fellow programmers that it's time to put down the pencils." Deploy to open a piece that reports a position the writer has already taken in public.
4. **The quoted strawman** - give the opponent's line in their own voice, then flatten it: "'But my opinion is as valid as yours!'. No, really, it's not." Deploy mid-argument to clear the standard objection.
5. **The category error** - "If you're stuck at 'AI is just another tool', you're making a category error." Deploy to reframe a debate by naming the wrong frame first.
6. **The inverted epigram** - Orwell bent to the subject: "See, all programmers are equal, but some programmers are more equal than others." Deploy once per piece, to mock a gatekeeping consensus.

### Lexicon
- Engineering-founder vocabulary: monolith, services, the cloud, renting versus owning, back of the napkin math, operations team, open source, agents, frontier models, omakase, the menu, the kitchen.
- Profanity and slang as spice: "most fucking sci-fi thing", "gawd damn parade", "delulu", "loser talk", "Bzzzzzzzzt!! Wrong!", "insanely beefy servers".
- Old-world grandeur in the same paragraph: "Nay, what a blessing", "The past is a foreign country", "the will to power", "sacred cows", "prepare the feast", "We may have killed God".
- Recurring benedictions: "What a time to be alive.", "relish the privilege of being alive during the days we taught the machines how to think", "This is the time to bloom."
- First-person plural for the company: "we at 37signals", "medium-sized companies like ours", "we're making our plans to leave."
- Pop-culture anchors used literally: The Terminator, Kubrick's HAL ("I'm sorry, Dave"), Orwell, DARPA, the Minneapolis City Council.

### Taboo list
- Never hedge. "I think" and "perhaps" appear only as the setup for a punch; "(mostly)" is as soft as it gets.
- Never a bulleted listicle in an essay; "#1 / #2" headers appear only when prescribing a numbered program.
- Never emoji in prose (one 🤘 on X is the ceiling); never hashtags.
- Never dooming. Pessimism about the present is "loser talk"; the posture is bullish on technology and on the reader.
- Never a summary paragraph. The last line is a dare, a blessing, or a wink.
- Never defer to "The Most Successful" as a reason to copy them; that is the exact error the monolith essay names.
- Never apologize for an opinionated design: "If most things on the menu disgust you, what on earth are you still doing at the table?"

### Punctuation and formatting
- Exclamation marks about once per three paragraphs, doubled or tripled for mock outrage ("Bzzzzzzzzt!! Wrong!"); parentheticals get their own exclamation ("(half of GDP and a third of all workers!)").
- ALL CAPS reserved for mock-shouting a strawman or a chant ("HOW ARE WE MAKING COMPUTERS DO THIS???"), roughly one post in ten.
- Numbers carried as receipts with units and periods: 90%, $1.5 million per year, half a million dollars, 43 reactors across 14 sites; then editorialized immediately ("Which is fine! But...").
- No dashes observed in the 32 sampled excerpts; asides sit in parentheses or in a short follow-on sentence (see Punctuation Override).
- Italics or bold almost never; emphasis comes from the short sentence after the long one.
- Headlines are the thesis or the joke: "Redundancy is the engine of progress", "Over my dead pencil", "Denmark desperately needs more inequality".

### POV and stance
- Progress is the default and redundancy is its engine; the right response to a technology shift is to get in, not to legislate it to a halt.
- Ownership over renting, monoliths over premature services, opinionated menus over infinite configuration: the same preference for fewer, stronger choices in every domain.
- The company is the proof: 37signals left the cloud, saved the money, kept the team the same size, and published the numbers.
- Bullish on AI agents as coworkers, not tools; contemptuous of gatekeeping about who counts as "a real programmer."
- Civilizational optimist with a European critic's eye: the West has lost its will to power and can get it back; "believing that 'it's over' is just loser talk."
- Addresses the reader directly and dares them: "You gotta get in there." / "We need you."

### Emotional register
- Hot, delighted, certain. The energy is real enthusiasm ("the most fucking sci-fi thing I've ever been privy to") rather than anger, even when he is swinging.
- Generous toward builders, operators, and anyone shipping; scornful toward doomers, bureaucrats, and consensus-by-prestige.
- Humor is the mock-shout, the sound effect, and the metaphor pushed one step too far ("How is next Thursday for you?").
- Not the calm shop-owner dryness of Jason Fried, not the cold civilizational essay of Peter Thiel, not the erudite insult of Nassim Taleb. He is the engineer-founder at the keynote podium, grinning, with the invoice in his hand.
- When a draft reads flat, raise the temperature by one exclamation and one sound effect; when it reads merely loud, add the receipt with a year and a dollar figure.

## Signature Moves

1. **Receipt first** - lead with a specific number carrying a date or a unit, then editorialize in the next sentence. Deploy as the first line of any argument.
2. **Settled thesis** - state the conclusion as already decided, in under ten words. Deploy as the opener of contrarian posts and the title of most.
3. **One governing metaphor** - pick a single image (a sushi menu, pencils, sacred cows, earthquake insurance) and carry it to the last sentence. Deploy once per piece; never mix two.
4. **Strawman in their voice** - quote the standard objection in the opponent's exact words, then flatten it with a short line. Deploy mid-argument.
5. **Setup, then punch** - a long sentence followed by a 2-6 word exclamatory sentence. Deploy as the paragraph-closing rhythm throughout.
6. **Slang beside scripture** - put "delulu" and "Nay, what a blessing" within a paragraph of each other. Deploy once or twice per post, never as a tic.
7. **The company as proof** - "we at 37signals" did the thing, here are the numbers, here is what did not change. Deploy in any build-in-public or operations piece.
8. **Benediction close** - end on a dare or a blessing in one short line: "What a time to be alive." / "We need you." Deploy as the last line of every piece.

## Playbooks

### Contrarian Take Generator
**Use when** the user wants to push against an industry consensus (cloud by default, microservices, AI doom, "real programmers", regulatory caution).
1. Get the consensus and the number that undercuts it. Without a real receipt (a cost, a percentage, a date) the take is just heat; ask, or mark [VERIFY].
2. Open with the receipt or the settled thesis in one or two sentences.
3. Quote the standard objection in the opponent's own voice, then flatten it in a short line.
4. Pick one governing metaphor and carry it through every paragraph.
5. Close on a dare or a benediction, one line, with a wink if the metaphor allows.
- Voice rules: one exclamation per 2-3 paragraphs; at most one profanity; one archaic flourish; no hedge; no bullets.
```
[Receipt with a date or unit, or settled thesis, 1-2 sentences]
[Why the consensus formed, stated fairly in 2-3 sentences]
["Quoted objection." Short flattening line.]
[Governing metaphor, developed over 2-3 paragraphs with one more number]
[Dare or benediction, one line]
```

### Ghostwriter
**Use when** the user wants a short essay, a company blog post, or a founder letter in this register under their own name.
1. Interview for the user's own position already taken in public (a talk, a decision, a shipped change) and the numbers behind it. The "scene from my week" opener must be true of the user.
2. Draft 300-900 words in 2-5 sentence paragraphs; every paragraph ends on a shorter sentence than it started with.
3. Insert one strawman quote and one governing metaphor; attach a receipt with a unit to the central claim.
4. Use "we" for the user's company and "you" for the reader; dare the reader at least once.
5. Edit pass: cut every hedge, every bullet, every summary sentence; convert any dash per the override; keep one or two exclamation marks that are earned.
- Voice rules: profanity optional and never more than one; slang and grandeur both present once; headline is the thesis or the joke.
```
[Title as thesis or joke]
[Scene from my week or dated receipt]
[Settled thesis]
[Objection in their voice, flattened]
[Metaphor paragraphs, one receipt each]
[Benediction or dare]
```

### Build-in-Public Writer
**Use when** the user wants to publish a decision and its results (an infrastructure change, a pricing move, a hiring choice) with real numbers.
1. Lead with the decision as a settled sentence ("So we're making our plans to leave.") or with the result ("The back of the napkin math is that we'll save at least $1.5 million per year").
2. Give the receipts: the before cost, the after cost, the time frame, and what did not change (team size, uptime).
3. Name the industry default being broken and why it made sense for others but not for "companies like ours."
4. Admit the one thing that was harder than expected, without softening the conclusion.
5. Close with the forward dare: what the user will publish next, or what the reader should try.
- Voice rules: every claim has a number; "we" throughout; one sound effect or mock-shout at most; no victory lap longer than one sentence.
```
[Decision or result as a settled sentence]
[Receipts: before, after, time frame, what stayed the same]
[Why the default exists and why it fails here]
[The hard part, one paragraph]
[Forward dare, one line]
```

## Anti-patterns

- Opening with context or a hedge instead of a receipt or a thesis. Sentence one carries the number or the verdict.
- A take with no number anywhere in it. Heat without a receipt is not this voice.
- Two metaphors in one piece, or a metaphor abandoned after the opener.
- Dooming, caution, or "it depends." The posture is bullish and decided.
- Calm, dry understatement throughout (that is Jason Fried's register); DHH turns the voltage up.
- Long subordinated civilizational prose with no exclamation (that is Peter Thiel's register); his sentences punch.
- Insulting a named individual with classical flourish (that is Nassim Taleb's register); DHH mocks consensus and institutions, not people by name.
- Over-imitation tells: an exclamation mark in every sentence; "Nay" twice in one post; a sound effect per paragraph; profanity more than once; "What a time to be alive" as the closer of every piece.
- Presenting his positions and stories (Rails is omakase, the majestic monolith, 37signals leaving the cloud and saving $1.5 million a year, "put down the pencils", the Denmark and Europe essays, his car collection) as the user's own. Credit them or build the user's own case.
- Emoji, hashtags, or bulleted tips in an essay body.

## Punctuation Override

No em or en dashes appear in the 32 sampled excerpts; DHH's asides go into parentheses that carry their own exclamation marks, and his interruptions become a short follow-on sentence ("Which is fine! But..."). The operator's global copy rule bans em and en dashes in all published output, so this voice needs no conversion of his own habit; when a draft reaches for a dash, use a parenthetical, a colon before the receipt, or a new short sentence, and let an exclamation mark carry the emphasis where it is earned. This is an intentional override applied to the operator's copy, not an error.

## Guardrails

- Never present output as actually authored by David Heinemeier Hansson.
- Never fabricate a quotation, a cost figure, a benchmark, or a company decision attributed to him or to 37signals. If a line is not in `references/voice-corpus.md` or freshly re-verified at its URL, mark it [VERIFY] rather than reconstructing it from memory.
- Items 1-32 are verbatim and self-written (HEY World, dhh.dk, Signal v. Noise, X). Items 101-110 are a documented spoken sample from one podcast (Lex Fridman #474, machine transcript); his keynotes are still unsampled, so treat spoken-style output as lightly grounded and label it when precision matters.
- The co-authored books (Rework, Remote, It Doesn't Have to Be Crazy at Work) were not sampled and are shared with Jason Fried; do not quote them as his alone.
- His political and cultural positions (Europe, Denmark, inequality, the Minneapolis City Council) are his. The skill borrows the polemic form; it does not put his positions in the user's mouth or generate advocacy for them as the user's own.
- Credit Orwell, Kubrick, and other borrowed lines as he does; credit the cloud-exit numbers to 37signals if they are cited.
- Ghostwriting is for the USER's own essays, company posts, and updates in an inspired-by style, under the user's own name.
- Refuse deceptive impersonation: fake 37signals or Rails affiliation, fake endorsements, or any "DHH reviewed this" claim.

## Output Format

Default: 3 variants plus one recommended pick with a one-line why. For essays, return the title, the opening receipt or thesis, and the governing metaphor first so the user can approve the shape before the body is written. For build-in-public posts, return the receipts table (before, after, time frame, unchanged) before the prose. Flag every number, date, and company claim as [VERIFY] unless it is the user's own or drawn directly from `references/voice-corpus.md`, because this voice's authority is the invoice being real.

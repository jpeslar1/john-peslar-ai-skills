## Verbatim excerpts (perell.com essays and notes)

1. "I was a terrible writer growing up. / I got a C- in my college writing class. At one point that semester, I skipped ten of those classes in a row because I didn't see the value in learning to write. When I told my high school writing teacher that I'd taught thousands of people to write online, she spit out her drink because she thought I was joking."
    The Ultimate Guide to Writing Online, perell.com, 2022-08-26, https://perell.com/essay/the-ultimate-guide-to-writing-online/, verbatim

2. "Five years later, I can say that writing on the Internet is among the best life decisions I've made. The 90 minutes I spend writing every morning is my most important habit and the activation code for just about everything good that happens to me."
    The Ultimate Guide to Writing Online, perell.com, 2022-08-26, https://perell.com/essay/the-ultimate-guide-to-writing-online/, verbatim

3. "Writing gave me wings. It unlocked the latent potential of the Internet. You can access humanity's best thinking, improve your own, and freely share your best ideas to a global audience. The writer's path is no longer reserved for authors, journalists, or your aunt's crazy Facebook rants. Anybody can walk it now."
    The Ultimate Guide to Writing Online, perell.com, 2022-08-26, https://perell.com/essay/the-ultimate-guide-to-writing-online/, verbatim

4. "Words are the atomic unit of the internet. / With the stroke of a pen, you can build your network, improve your thinking, and create opportunities for yourself."
    Why You Should Write, perell.com, 2019-07-22, https://perell.com/essay/why-you-should-write/, verbatim

5. "Writing is like weightlifting for the brain. Just as you'll improve your food diet if you start cooking, you'll improve your information diet if you start writing. Testing the limits of your ideas is the fastest way to improve them and raise your intelligence."
    Why You Should Write, perell.com, 2019-07-22, https://perell.com/essay/why-you-should-write/, verbatim

6. "An empty white page is a mirror into your mind. When the ideas in your mind are clouded, so are the words on the page in front of you. Re-writing is re-thinking."

7. "Jerry Garcia once said: "You want to be the only person who does what you do." / The ultimate goal of writing online is to build a Personal Monopoly. It's your unique intersection of skills, interests, and personality traits where you can be known as the best thinker on a topic and open yourself up to the serendipity that makes writing online so special."
    Build a Personal Monopoly, perell.com (note), 2020-10-27, https://perell.com/note/build-a-personal-monopoly/, verbatim

8. "If your Personal Monopoly isn't immediately obvious, that's okay. Most people take five years to find theirs. As you search, double down on the ideas that captivate you, the compliments people give you, and the projects you enjoy working on."
    Build a Personal Monopoly, perell.com (note), 2020-10-27, https://perell.com/note/build-a-personal-monopoly/, verbatim

9. "I deplore the phrase: "Find Your Niche." / It's always felt too passive. It assumes that a niche is like an Easter Egg that you'll find if you look hard enough. But the most interesting ideas don't yet have names."
    Don't Find a Niche. Create One., perell.com (note), 2020-10-21, https://perell.com/note/dont-find-a-niche-create-one/, verbatim

10. "The novelist Raymond Chandler used to write on index cards on his typewriter. / He had one rule: something had to happen on every notecard. By following the rule, his stories moved at a brisk pace. For Chandler, it trained him to be crisp and direct. The online writing equivalent is to make sure your reader has an epiphany every 250 words."
    The Raymond Chandler Rule, perell.com (note), 2020-10-09, https://perell.com/note/the-raymond-chandler-rule/, verbatim

11. "What was once a "need" has become a "want." / I no longer go because I need to. I go because I want to. / Writing is similar."

12. "Consistency comes before choice. / Like the gym, you can do whatever you want once you fall in love with the habit. The luxury of only writing when you feel like it is something you have to earn."

## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~14 words in essays, with a long explanatory sentence followed by a 3-6 word aphorism ("Re-writing is re-thinking.", "Consistency comes before choice."); spoken sentences loosen to 20+ words with "like" and "Do you know what I mean?".
- One-line paragraphs: roughly 45% of essay and note paragraphs; the opening line is a standalone paragraph in nearly every piece sampled.
- Hook-type frequency across the 28 written excerpts: confession or self-implicating admission ~30%, vivid concrete image or named character ~25%, named-term coinage or definition ~20%, attributed quote from a famous figure ~15%, flat contrarian claim ("I deplore the phrase") ~10%.
- Named coinages in Title Case: Personal Monopoly, Hugging the X-Axis, the Originality Disease, the Raymond Chandler Rule, Imitate then Innovate, Expression Is Compression, the Paradox of Reading; "I call it the ..." is the recurring construction.
- Attributed quotes: one per essay or note on average (Jerry Garcia, Raymond Chandler, Jason Fried), always followed by a gloss or "The online writing equivalent is...".
- Extended metaphors from crafts and the body: sap to syrup, weightlifting for the brain, the blank page as a mirror, a constellation within a galaxy; each developed for a full paragraph, not dropped as a one-liner.
- Exclamation marks: zero; he bans them on principle (item 23).
- Emoji: zero; hashtags: zero; "the Internet" capitalized.
- Profanity: zero in the sampled corpus.
- Hedges: few in essays ("that's okay", "I suspect" style softeners around advice); heavy in speech ("I basically think", "Like, I'm like", "Now, there are obvious exceptions.").
- Numbers: sparse and illustrative rather than financial: 90 minutes every morning, a C- grade, ten skipped classes, 50 gallons of sap, 250 words per epiphany, 100 books per year at 3x speed, six years of teaching.
- Favorite openers: a one-line confession ("I was a terrible writer growing up."), a named character in a scene ("Mike boastfully reads 100 books per year."), a flat declaration ("Words are the atomic unit of the internet."), or an attributed quote.
- Favorite closers: a short aphorism that compresses the essay ("Re-writing is re-thinking.", "Anybody can walk it now.", "Don't compensate."); never a call to action.
- Spoken register (official and documented transcripts): same love of analogy ("constellation of stars", "seed stage investor in ideas"), more hedged and more willing to label a view a hot take; cites Jason Fried by name mid-answer.

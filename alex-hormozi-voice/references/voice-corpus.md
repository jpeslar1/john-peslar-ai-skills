## Derived stats (estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~11 words (estimate).
- One-line paragraphs: on social, ~90% of paragraphs are a single sentence (estimate).
- Hook-type frequency (estimate): comparison beats ~30%, definition reframes ~20%, binaries ~20%, brutal standards ~15%, math proofs ~15%.
- Emoji usage: CORRECTED 2026-10-18 from 100 of his own X posts: rare and functional, not zero (🧵 to mark a thread, ☝🏼, ✊🏽, ☠️ once). ALL CAPS: absent. Exclamation marks: zero in 100 posts, confirmed. Casual spelling tolerated ("theres", "arent", "gonna", "gotta"); he does not self-correct typos.
- Line breaks: on X he breaks at every clause and leaves a blank line between beats; the line break itself does rhythmic work and should be preserved.
- Favorite openers: bare nouns as subjects ("A lead...", "A sale...", "The job of the CEO:"), "The goal is to...", and the dominant 2024-2026 template "You can beat 99% of people…" (six of 100 timeline posts). Related templates: "How to stay poor:", "Dear younger me:", "Brutal business truth:".
- Favorite closers: compressed rule or exchange-rate statement; never a question.
- Theme shift (verified): the 2024-2026 X feed is overwhelmingly endurance, identity, and suffering ("Suffering is a fixed cost of life"); "math over motivation" describes the videos, not the current feed.
- Spoken register is a distinct dialect (items 122-133): long run-ons joined by "And so" and "right?", the habitual "the thing is is that", numbered scaffolding ("Business truth number N" then a one-sentence headline then a story about a named founder), borrowed authority from Musk, Bezos, and Truett Cathy.
- Sales doctrine (2021 video, items 131-133): only three kinds of statement in a close (questions, restatements, short stories); never teach on a sales call; do not pitch until the yes is certain; "closers ask hard questions"; the job is to break beliefs.

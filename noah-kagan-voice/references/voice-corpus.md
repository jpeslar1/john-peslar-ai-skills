## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~9 words in blog prose; a quarter of sentences are 3-6 words ('Holy shit.', 'I agree with them.', 'So I'm practicing.').
- One-line paragraphs: about half of the 2026 blog paragraphs are a single sentence; each 2026 post opens with a 'The short version:' summary block, then story.
- Hook-type frequency across the 30 written items: blunt confession ('Three Sundays ago I did not want to go to work on Monday.') ~30%, chiasmus or near-rhyme rule ('Think NOW, not HOW.') ~20%, story then one-line rule ~20%, big self-disclosed number ($100M a year; return buyers down 40%) ~20%, direct question to the reader ('Can I be real with you? Real real?') ~10%.
- ALL CAPS one- or two-word emphasis: 9 instances in 30 written items (A LOT twice, HUGE, STAY, BEST, NOW, HOW, plus the stretched 'Boooring'); never a whole line.
- Exclamation marks: 1 in 30 written items, and it is parenthetical ('(twice!)'). Emphasis is caps, not exclamation.
- Profanity and crude humor: 6 in 30 written items ('pain in the ass', 'Holy shit.', 'Oh shit, now I'm accountable.', 'shit-canned', 'boner hard', 'it sucked'); zero in the ASR sample. Softened interjections also appear ('holy schnikeys', 'Yea').
- Emoji and kaomoji: 1 shrug kaomoji in 30 written items; the blog's recurring headphones marker on the podcast link line was not excerpted.
- Hedges: near zero. He self-corrects instead ('That's an exaggeration, but I can take more risk'), which keeps the claim and adds the caveat in the same breath.
- Numbers: specific and self-disclosing in ~40% of items ($100M revenue, 20+ failed startups, 10 people in a sales meeting, 8 businesses, 2,640 billionaires, 834 years, a $100,000 paycheck). Never a vague 'a lot of money'.
- Analogies: food and household in 4 of 30 ('a good burger recipe', 'adding to the menu', 'Business Marriage', 'Diners, Drive-Ins and Dives'); named asides to real people in 2 ('Get that in, Jason.', 'holler at me').
- Favorite openers: a one-sentence confession; a big number followed by 'But before that:'; 'Can I be real with you?'; 'I know what you're thinking.'
- Favorite closers: a bolded one-line rule, often chiasmus ('Intensity gets the ball rolling. Sustainability gets the ball winning.'); 'Lesson learned:'; 'That's it.'; spoken sign-off 'Uncle Noah loves you'.
- Spoken register (ASR sample, 4 items): big-number hook with a personal 1% claim, imperative lesson titles ('You Must Be Your Own Boss'), casual math out loud ('834 years just to make a billion'), like-and-subscribe close; no filler words survive in the captions sampled.

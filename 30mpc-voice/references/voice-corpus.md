## Structural notes for the writer

- The war story always carries counts. 800 dials, 0 meetings, 40 dials, 3 meetings, 300M calls, 3x.
- Scripts are visually separated and written as speech, never as a summary of what to say.
- The joke lives in parentheses. The body stays tactical.
- The piece ends in a checklist phrased as questions the reader runs on their own draft.
- Frameworks borrowed from podcast guests are credited by name and episode number.
- Farrokh writes the comedic story-led issues, Cegelski the structural ones. Both use the same skeleton.

**Spoken register (added 2026-10-18 from items 101+):**
- Every tactic is performed, not described: "Here's how that sounds. Ring ring." then a two-sided role-play with the prospect voiced in character, then "So what I did there is…". The written corpus prints scripts; the spoken register acts them.
- Openers: Armand opens with the war story and counts (200 calls a week, four weeks, zero meetings). Nick opens with a domestic scene unrelated to sales and reveals the lesson a minute in. Solo tactic episodes open "Folks," with a stat in the first sentence.
- Closers: a recap that calls back the opening image, then a "steal this" CTA to the free resource (18 objections guide, the course).
- Spoken pet phrases: "Just so no one from my team calls you again", "Shoot, this one's totally on me", "Would you be completely against…", "Sell the test drive, not the car", "pattern interrupt", "telemarketer stigma", "kick rocks", "problem proposition", "chain objection handling"; direct address as "sellers" or "folks".
- Comedy aloud is absurd specifics inside the script ("wait like 75 years for a squat rack", "eucalyptus towels"), not parenthetical asides. Armand is the louder comic; Nick's humor lives in the story setup.
- Spoken vs written: longer sentences joined with "and so" and "so notice"; "gonna", "gotta", fragments; numbers drift between tellings (three vs four meetings), so treat spoken stats as anecdote; hosts credit each other by first name mid-explanation.

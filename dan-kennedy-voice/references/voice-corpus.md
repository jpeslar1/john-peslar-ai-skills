**Authorship warning, read this.** Items 1-25 were read directly from magneticmarketing.com on the research date and are verbatim text, but that site publishes his methodology under the Magnetic Marketing brand, which has changed ownership, and the posts do not carry a personal byline. **Treat them as the doctrine in the house register rather than as words Dan Kennedy personally typed**, and treat their label as `documented`, not `verified` (see the Provenance revisions section below for why this is now stated explicitly rather than left as a caveat only). ## Structural notes for the writer

- The punch sentence is the signature. Two to six words, standing alone, delivering the verdict. "It was moving it." "It's sloppy pricing." "It's a race you won't win."
- Trades are named, never generalised: dentists, chiropractors, restaurants, music schools, childcare centres, financial advisors.
- Money is always exact and always doing argumentative work: $299 against $89, $150 against $400, 800 words against $80,000.
- Parallel contrasts arrive in threes: clicks against customers, opens against orders, engagement against revenue.
- Section headers are claims the reader can disagree with.
- Numbered questions let the owner audit their own business, which is the doctrine's delivery mechanism.
- In the verified, personally bylined material (items 36-49), he writes in first person about a single real, anonymized example (the Tina/BMW ad) and sustains it across several paragraphs rather than switching examples every sentence. The house-register posts (items 1-25) almost never sustain one example this long. Treat the extended real-example teardown as part of the authentic register, not just the punch-sentence rhythm.

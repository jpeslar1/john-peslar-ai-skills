# Ship 30 (Nicolas Cole and Dickie Bush) Voice Corpus

Research date: 2026-10-19

The excerpts below are short quotations from public posts, articles, and talks, included under fair use for style analysis and attribution. They are third-party content and NOT covered by this repository's MIT license.

**Provenance note.** All 38 items are verbatim and each carries its speaker. Items 1-10 and 38 are co-authored (11 items): essays from the Ship 30 for 30 blog and Start Writing Online newsletter archive bylined "Dickie Bush & Nicolas Cole", read from the live Webflow pages. Items 11-25 are Dickie Bush alone (15 items): a solo-bylined Ship 30 blog post on thread hooks, his own X posts (@dickiebush, 2021-2023) fetched individually from the syndication tweet-result endpoint with timestamps, and his Substack essays (2025). Items 26-37 are Nicolas Cole alone (12 items): his own X posts (@Nicolascole77, 2021-2023) from the same endpoint, his Substack "The Art & Business of Writing" (2024-2026), and his own on-blog list of quotes from his 2020 book, which is the only book text sampled. Ship 30 blog posts carry no publish date on the page; the dates given are sitemap lastmod values and may postdate first publication. Nothing here comes from a quote aggregator. No spoken material was collected: no podcast or video transcript of either writer was sampled, and the paid Write With AI newsletter was not used.

**Punctuation note.** Several excerpts preserve em dashes (items 1, 4, 18, 24) and en dashes (items 11, 17, 21) because they are verbatim quotations; both writers use dashes constantly in their own copy. Items 19 and 26 keep their emoji (🚢, 👇). The operator's copy rule still bans em and en dashes in anything generated in this voice; see the Punctuation Override in SKILL.md.

## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~11 words; X lines and stacked fragments pull the median down, Cole's Substack paragraphs run longer (15-20 words) but still break one idea per line.
- One-line paragraphs: roughly 70% of all lines in the corpus; nearly 100% on X; co-authored blog posts alternate one-liners with 2-3 sentence paragraphs.
- Hook-type frequency across the 38 excerpts: credibility number plus colon promise ~30% ("500 hours", "100 hours", "over 500 business books"), silent controversial opinion or brutal truth ~25%, numbered how-to list ~20%, personal origin scene ~15%, flat rule or maxim ~10%.
- Colon-terminated promises: present in most X openers ("Here they are for free:", "Here are 10 of them you probably know nothing about:", "answer these 5 questions:").
- Repeated stems: at least one stacked repetition per long piece ("That means nights. / That means weekends."; "And my first $100. / And my first $1,000."; "Hit publish. Then hit publish again. And again. And again.").
- Exclamation marks: rare in Cole's essays (one in item 37), near zero in Bush's X posts; ritual newsletter lines ("Ahoy and happy Monday!") carry them.
- Emoji: occasional, one per post at most (🚢 as brand mark, 👇 as pointer, a flag in Dickie's location stamps); hashtags: zero.
- Caps for emphasis: one word per piece ("KING", "*entire*" in asterisks, "Boom. Done."); category terms capitalized as proper nouns (Digital Writer, Online Writing, Shippers).
- Profanity: minimal; one censored title ("Not Giving a Fck") and "brutal" as the preferred intensifier.
- Hedges: near zero; both write in absolutes ("If the reader is confused, they're going.", "Subjectivity doesn't scale.", "Nobody is going to make it happen for you.").
- Numbers: dollar-denominated and specific: $120,000, $20M, $30,000,000, $1M per month, 732 followers, 5,600 Shippers, 47-tabs-open, 500 books, 99%.
- Favorite openers: a bold declarative then a credibility line (Bush); "Dear Writer," and a broke-in-Chicago origin scene (Cole); "We have a saying in Ship 30 for 30" (co-authored).
- Favorite closers: an imperative stack ("Hit publish. Then hit publish again."), a flat maxim ("Subjectivity doesn't scale.", "You document for yourself."), or the ritual sign-off ("Chat next week!").
- Spoken vs. written: all items are written; Bush's Substack reads as spoken notes (dashes, no terminal periods), Cole's as polished essay. No podcast sample exists in this corpus.

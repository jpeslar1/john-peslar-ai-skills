## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~10 words in newsletter and blog prose; many sentences are 3-6 words ('Less is more.', 'One thing, baby.'); X posts run 15-35 words as a single lowercase sentence.
- One-line paragraphs: roughly 40% of newsletter and blog lines; the Loom post stacks three one-line 'They never talked about' clauses as separate lines.
- Hook-type frequency across the 24 written items: numbered hot take ~20%, contrarian flip ('That's backwards', 'Couldn't be less true') ~20%, big-money receipt ($1B with 13 employees, $975M in CASH) ~20%, concrete fantasy scene (quiet Slack, 'cha-ching' channel) ~15%, confession of a miss (Loom pre-seed) ~10%, question to the reader ~15%.
- Repetition triplets: 3 in 24 written items ('basically free' x3; 'They never talked about' x3; 'Most founders / Most companies / Most products').
- Ellipses: 3 in 24 written items, used for comic pause ('boring...boring', 'ouch').
- ALL CAPS single-word emphasis: 4 in 24 written items (CASH, MILLIONS, CLEAR, ANYONE); never a whole line.
- Emoji: 1 in 24 written items as a section marker (the chili on the hot-takes letter); the research notes recurring newsletter markers (a heart on 'Greg's bookmarks', a wave) that were not excerpted.
- Profanity: zero in the sampled excerpts. Hedges: 2 ('I think' in one X post, 'probably' once); the newsletter voice asserts, the X voice muses.
- Numbers: concrete dollar figures with casual math in ~40% of items ($25k could have become $3M-$4M; $100 and an audience beats $1M in 2010; 5,000 likes; 131M+ impressions). Round, memorable, never a decimal.
- Favorite openers: 'This might be the most important post I've written in a while.'; 'People are saying X. Couldn't be less true.'; 'Everyone says X. That's backwards.'; on X, 'imagine ...' setup then punchline.
- Favorite closers: a 2-4 word slogan ('Less is more.', 'Bottom line > top line.', 'One thing!!'), a question back ('Anyone else?', 'How cool is that?'), or an imperative ('Start writing it down.'); spoken sign-off 'Take care.'
- Spoken register (ASR sample, 5 items): fillers 'um' and 'like' are frequent; 'If you're new here, I'm Greg Isenberg' intro; 'If this episode gets 5,000 likes' CTA; 'alpha' and 'creative juices flowing' as pet words; sentences run longer and loop back on themselves.
- Written vs X: newsletter and blog use standard capitalization and punctuation; the four X posts are 3 of 4 all-lowercase with no terminal punctuation, two-line setup/punchline form.

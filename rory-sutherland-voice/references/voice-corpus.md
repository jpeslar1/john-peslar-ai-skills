# Rory Sutherland Voice Corpus

Research date: 2026-08-24; primary-source re-verification and refresh 2026-10-18

The excerpts below are short quotations from public posts/interviews, included under fair use for style analysis and attribution. They are third-party content and NOT covered by this repository's MIT license.

**Provenance note.** This corpus was built 2026-08-24, before this repo's verified/documented labeling convention existed. Despite the "Verified excerpts" heading below (kept as originally written), every item is sourced from Goodreads' author-quotes page, a TED talk transcript archive, or a documented compilation of his lines, not a primary text or recording fetched and confirmed by Claude this session. Item 20 is explicitly marked secondhand in its own note. Under the current convention, treat every item here as `documented`, not `verified`.

**Re-verification against primary sources (2026-10-18).** Each of items 1-20 was checked against his own columns, talks, and posts. Result: 1 verified exactly (item 14, 2011 TED caption track), 3 found as a primary variant with different wording (item 1: his 2016 TVOL1000 self-authored profile reads "weed with a marketing budget"; item 13 is a blend of the 2009 and 2011 TED wordings; item 18 appears in a 2017 One One One interview as "whatever you do in life, try and…"), and 16 not found in any primary source (the Alchemy and Wiki Man book lines, plus items 15 and 20, which are paraphrases with no traceable origin). Items 1-20 stay in this file as documented, aggregator-sourced material and must NOT be quoted in client copy. Quote from items 101-135 below instead, which are his own words fetched from the Spectator and from TED caption tracks.

## Derived stats (estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~22 words, the longest of any voice in this repo; punchlines drop to 6 to 10.
- One-line paragraphs: rare (~10%); he writes in 3 to 6 sentence prose paragraphs.
- Hook-type frequency: aphoristic inversions and costed absurdities dominate his most-quoted material; institutional confessions next.
- Emoji, hashtags, ALL CAPS: zero. Parentheticals: frequent. Genuine questions: roughly one per extended passage.
- Favorite openers: CORRECTED 2026-10-18 from the 13 fetched columns. The first sentence is a concrete anecdote or a dry observation every time ("My father once…", "When I joined…", "I once asked…"), never a rhetorical question. The "Why is it necessary to…" opener in the aggregator items is not how he opens a column.
- Favorite closers: a one-line kicker joke, often a costed or ranked absurdity or a deliberately provocative policy proposal, that inverts the setup. Confirmed across items 109, 112, 115, 120, 121.
- Register: educated British conversational, amused, concrete artifacts (trains, wine, taxis, flowers) anchoring every abstraction. CORRECTED: ruder than the aggregator items suggest. He reaches for a vulgar noun exactly where a lesser writer would reach for an abstraction ("witless tosser", "sodding houses", "don't drive like a git", "absolute shite" in the 2009 TED). Flat polite delivery around the vulgarity is the joke.
- Current targets (2026 columns): institutions, lawyers versus engineers, "defensive decision-making", bureaucracy; marketing is now the lens, not the target.
- Borrowed authority: nearly every column hangs on one sentence attributed to a named friend or expert (Guru Madhavan, Stephen Wolfram, Mark Bostock, Richard Thaler) and builds on it. Missing from the first-pass corpus.
- Column mechanics (verified): ~20-25 words per sentence, a parenthetical aside roughly every other paragraph, one standalone pull-quote sentence per column.
- Spoken vs written: TED delivery uses "Now, …" as a paragraph marker and "actually" as filler several times a minute; the columns use neither. Add the tics for spoken output, strip them for a column.

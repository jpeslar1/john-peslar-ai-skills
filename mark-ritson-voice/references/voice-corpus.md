# Mark Ritson Voice Corpus

Research date: 2026-10-19

The excerpts below are short quotations from public posts, articles, and talks, included under fair use for style analysis and attribution. They are third-party content and NOT covered by this repository's MIT license.

**Provenance note.** Items 1-28 are verbatim: copied from the full bodies of eleven Marketing Week columns he wrote under his own byline between 2019 and 2025. Post-2020 columns were fetched directly with a plain desktop-UA request and returned complete text; the one 2019 column (item 11 and item 12) truncated at the paywall after paragraph six, so only its visible paragraphs are quoted. Items 29-32 are verbatim from four of his own LinkedIn posts, read from each permalink's page metadata with the publish date taken from the page's JSON-LD. Items 33-35 are DOCUMENTED, not verbatim: they are direct quotes from his 2021 Festival of Marketing talk as transcribed by a Marketing Week journalist, so wording is the reporter's record of his speech. Nothing here comes from a quote aggregator. His Mini MBA course material, Marketing Week video segments, and podcast appearances were not sampled. Profanity inside quotes is his.

**Punctuation note.** Three excerpts preserve spaced en dashes because they are verbatim quotations (items 7, 16, 25); the en dash with spaces is his native aside marker. The operator's copy rule still bans em and en dashes in anything generated in this voice; see the Punctuation Override in SKILL.md.

## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~14 words in columns, pulled down by fragment chains of 1-4 words ("Flawless.", "As. We. Know. It."); the long setup sentences run 35-60 words.
- One-line paragraphs: roughly a quarter of column paragraphs; most are 2-4 sentences. LinkedIn posts are almost entirely one- or two-line paragraphs.
- Hook-type frequency across the 11 columns: you-have-been-told-this setup ~30%, the column-about-nothing or meta-opener ~20%, number-shrinks-the-hype ~20%, unpopular-perspective flat statement ~15%, absurd analogy up front ~15%.
- Profanity: 1-3 instances per column, placed at the point of maximum certainty (fucking, shit, shitfuckery, dogshit, horseshit, cock, wanker, fuck all); never in the opening sentence.
- Rhetorical questions: 1-3 per column, often in a volley ("Have any of the development team actually been in a meeting before?").
- Analogies: one British-colloquial or sporting analogy per argument (left-footed footballers, midfielders vs Manchester United, barista and kombucha, Roger Federer, Casanova).
- Numbers: one or two hard figures per column used to deflate hype (92% not advertising, $263m vs Coke every 72 hours, 3,000 words, $600,000 on research, 32 years); no decimals.
- Emoji: zero. Hashtags: zero in columns, rare on LinkedIn. Caps for emphasis: rare, used as a shouted stage direction ("LOOK at it").
- Hedges: almost none in columns ("I suspect" once); "I think" appears mainly in the spoken sample.
- Sentences starting with And or But: several per column; it is his main connective.
- Favorite openers: what you have recently been told by a vendor or agency; a column that announces it is about nothing; a number that makes the fad look small.
- Favorite closers: a flat imperative or a one-line payoff ("Do the best you can. Work hard. And don't be so hard on yourself."; "And usually untrained. And defensive."), never a summary.
- Spoken register (journalist transcription): same stance and analogies, softer profanity, more "I think" and "we", bracketed asides to the room; written columns are tighter and ruder.

## What the corpus rests on

Items 1-30 of the first corpus section come from public newsletter issues, two interviews, and one tweet, fetched as public pages; anyone can re-check them at their URLs. The larger and richer half is the "Email newsletter excerpts" section: 15 full Big Desk Energy issues and 5 beehiiv product emails that Tyler sent to the operator's inbox between April and October 2026, copied verbatim from the delivered email. This is primary source in the strictest sense (the author wrote it, the author sent it, nothing passed through a journalist or an aggregator), and it is where the structural detail in this skill comes from: subject-line patterns, the fixed share ask, segment order, sponsor-block placement, closer rhythm, the off-cycle promo register. Most BDE essays are also published at mail.bigdeskenergy.com/p/<slug>, so a public copy usually exists; the product emails exist only as delivered email.

Being privately held is an access detail, not a quality problem. The one practical consequence is that a third party cannot click a link to re-check an inbox-sourced line; they need the inbox or the public slug. The refresh instructions below cover re-pulling.

## Pattern notes

### 1. Receipts-first transparency
Exact numbers everywhere: round sizes, hours to oversubscription, spend comparisons. The skill should demand the user's real figures and refuse to fake precision.

### 2. Absurdity acknowledgment
Milestones come with a self-aware beat about how ridiculous the game is. This is his humble-brag antidote and a required element of milestone posts in this voice.

### 3. Playbook reveal
After doing a notable thing (announcing a fundraise, running a launch), he publishes the numbered how-to. The skill should convert user events into copyable playbooks.

### 4. Bravado plus vulnerability
Flat confident claims ("We're going to win because no one cares...as much as I do") coexist with raw mental-health honesty in the same body of work. The voice needs both over time.

### 5. Ownership stance
Anti-platform-dependence arguments (the Substack teardown) made with evidence and vivid analogy, not sneering. Competitor critique always carries an argument.

### 6. Newsletter furniture
Fixed recurring segments (Tracks of the Week, curated links, reader interaction, fixed sign-off) that build habit. The Newsletter Writer playbook mirrors this structure.

### 7. Steelman then verdict (added 2026-10-18)
Opinion essays spend their first quarter stating the opposing thesis fairly and with its best evidence ("The thesis:", a HubSpot financials paragraph), then land one verdict sentence carrying the essay's single profanity, then stack receipts. The Contrarian Take playbook should enforce the steelman before the verdict.

### 8. Provocation, then "Let me explain…" (added 2026-10-18)
Three of nine fall-2026 essays open with a one- or two-sentence provocation ("I'm a dad!", "he'd have thought it was 2030", "Being a founder is pretty ass backwards sometimes") followed by "Let me explain…" on its own line. It is now a signature opener alongside the clipped-declarative pair.

# Alex Garcia Voice Corpus

Research date: 2026-10-19

The excerpts below are short quotations from public posts, articles, and talks, included under fair use for style analysis and attribution. They are third-party content and NOT covered by this repository's MIT license.

**Provenance note.** All 32 items are verbatim. Items 1-17 are copied from the raw HTML of first-person case studies and the site-wide footer on marketingexamined.com, his own newsletter archive (Next.js site; the author field in each page's __NEXT_DATA__ reads Alex Garcia or alexgarcia_atx, and dates come from the same block). Third-person brand profiles credited to him but reading as staff-written were excluded; only first-person posts are quoted. Items 18-27 are his own X posts (@alexgarcia_atx): root tweets were fetched and dated via fxtwitter, and mid-thread tweets came from Thread Reader unrolls of those same threads (marked inline; they rest on Thread Reader's copy, not an individual re-fetch). Items 28-32 are LinkedIn posts whose permalinks render logged out; text is from the page meta description and dates from JSON-LD. Nothing here comes from a quote aggregator. No spoken sample was collected: his podcast appearances (The Marketing Millennials ep. 64, Founder Stories) were located but not fetched, so the corpus is written-voice only. Emoji inside quotes are his.

**Punctuation note.** No em or en dashes appear in any excerpt. His dash is a spaced hyphen (" - ", items 9, 13, 24, 29), and he writes sequences and before/after shifts as arrows ("→", items 11 and 20); both are preserved as verbatim. The operator's copy rule still bans em and en dashes in anything generated in this voice; see the Punctuation Override in SKILL.md.

## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~9 words in case studies; fragments of 1-4 words are common ("Post. Post. Post. And post again." / "Tease. Create curiosity. Fomo."). X and LinkedIn run ~11 words per sentence.
- One-line paragraphs: roughly 60% of case-study lines are a single sentence or fragment; a 3-4 sentence paragraph is the ceiling.
- Hook-type frequency across the 32 excerpts: number-led proof claim ~25% ($3k into $150M, ~4 sec, 90/10, 200k, $40M); "Here's how to…" promise ending on a colon ~25%; consensus flip ("People say X. But really…") ~15%; named framework or label (3Ps, The Formula, 7 archetypes, media funnel) ~20%; personal disclosure or founder note ~15%.
- Arrow chains: present in 2 of 32 excerpts ("Refine → Scale → Optimize → Scale", "Oh, that's me → Oh, I need that in my life"); the research notes a Newsletter → Article → Thread → Podcast chain as a recurring device, roughly one per long piece.
- Emoji: 1 in 32 excerpts (👇🏽 on an X thread root); zero in the case studies. Hashtags: zero.
- Caps for emphasis: none; Title Case section headlines and funnel acronyms (TOF/MOF/BOF) are the only capitals.
- Profanity: one mild instance ("scale the hell out of it"). Slang and contractions are frequent ("gotta", "ppl", "whack").
- Hedges: near zero. "I like to think of it as" appears once; otherwise flat declaratives and imperatives ("Don't complicate it.").
- Numbers: big round proof figures and simple ratios rather than decimals ($3k, $150M, $40M, 200k, 90% / 10% / 5%, ~4 sec, 8-10 figure brands, 7 archetypes, 3Ps). No decimals, no footnoted stats.
- Favorite openers: "Two years of building…" (time-in-the-work), "People say…" (consensus flip), "In three years this brand turned…" (proof claim then "The brand?" reveal), "There are 7…" (count promise), "Don't make this mistake…".
- Favorite closers: a one-line kicker ("Never the other way around.", "Do what you want with this info.", "Just mute the word on your timeline"), a colon-ending promise whose payoff is the thread or post body ("Here's how to make one:"), and in case studies a numbered "So to help…" recap.
- Labels and lists: "The Formula:" as a takeaway label; "•" bullets on LinkedIn; numbered "1." items with a Title Case claim then 2-3 supporting lines on X.
- Spoken vs written: no spoken sample. X and LinkedIn are the newsletter voice compressed, with the same labels and kickers; treat them as one register.

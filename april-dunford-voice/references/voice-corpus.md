## Structural notes for the writer

- The war story always carries exact counts. 100 customers, 94, 6, 200 copies, $100, 60 percent capacity. Round numbers with no source are off-voice.
- Section headers are claims, not labels. "Status Quo Wins One Deal in Four" beats "Competition".
- She names her sources: Clayton Christensen, Jobs To Be Done, Lenny's newsletter, Building a Story Brand.
- Light profanity appears about once per long post and is always in service of compression.
- Dialogue is a real formatting device. When telling a customer story she writes it as a transcript.
- LinkedIn short-form (verified 2026-10-18, items 101-127) runs in two modes: (a) a one- or two-sentence standalone joke or aphorism with no link and no hashtags, her highest-engagement posts; (b) a 150-400 word teaser that compresses a newsletter or podcast episode into an emoji-bulleted list and ends "link in the comments", often lifted near-verbatim from the Substack.
- In short-form, emoji are structural, never decorative: 1️⃣2️⃣3️⃣ for steps, ⛔ for roadblocks, 🐣 vs 🦅 to split startup vs enterprise bullets, ⭐ to bracket the takeaway. They appear only at the start of a bullet. The long-form blog never does this.
- Self-deprecation is the short-form warmth layer ("I'm OLD - like seriously, really frikkin old", "small-town gal", "I have no idea, man"); the blog is confident and instructive.
- Dialogue-as-hook shortens on LinkedIn to a single quoted exec line, then her answer. The exact-counts habit survives (250ish workshops, 25 years, 100,000 copies, 14-page workbook).
- Vocabulary since 2025: "differentiated value" is the central term; the five components are now Competitive Alternatives, Distinct Capabilities, Differentiated Value, Best-Fit Customers, Market Category; also "pessimistic product thinking", "Investor Pitch Creep", "value themes", "vibing our way through it". AI (Anthropic, OpenAI) is the current frame; the thesis is unchanged.
- CTAs are soft and conversational: "Link in the comments", "What do you think? Let me know in the comments", "see you there?", discount codes offered plainly.

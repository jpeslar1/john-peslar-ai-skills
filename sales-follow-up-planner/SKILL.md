# Sales Follow-up Planner

Keep a legitimate deal moving with relevant next steps. Start from the actual thread and commitments. Do not imply that silence means interest or rejection with certainty.

## Classify the thread

Use the last messages and dates, stage, buyer's stated pain, outstanding question, promised next step, explicit timing, and prior attempts. Distinguish an unread cold opener, an active evaluation, a waiting-on-approver deal, a requested future check-in, and a closed/no-contact thread.

If the buyer said no or asked not to be contacted, close the sequence. A restart requires new permission or an appropriate inbound request. If they named a future date, respect it. An absent thread means a generic template, not fabricated relationship context.

## Build a finite sequence

Prefer a few useful touches with a stated final attempt. As a configurable default for an open deal with no agreed schedule, suggest a relevant recap, a new useful clarification a few business days later, and a respectful close-out roughly a week after that. Explain that timing is a proposed operating choice, not a published educator rule. Override it with the buyer's commitments and context.

Every touch needs a purpose: answer an open concern, share a relevant artifact, simplify a decision, or clarify whether to close the loop. Do not write "just checking in", guilt, fake urgency, invented case studies, or claims that the prospect consumed content without evidence.

Write actual drafts using the supplied facts. For each give intended date or trigger, new value, single question, and the event that cancels the next touch. Stop the sequence on any reply and handle its content before planning another send. Do not move to another channel to bypass a refusal.

A soft close-out should allow a no: "Would you prefer I close this out for now? Happy to revisit if [their real trigger] changes." Do not presume the trigger if unknown.

For a dormant lead, identify a relevant new event or an agreed revisit date. Do not schedule an automatic generic revival of every silent contact. For an internal operator, tie reminders to the sponsor's decision or resource milestone.

## Deliver

- Evidence-based thread status and missing context.
- Two or three tailored drafts as appropriate, with dates/triggers and stop rules.
- A reply tree for interested, later, concern, no, and continued silence.
- A record of the next owner/action/date and final close-out condition.

On review, count real replies and mutually agreed next steps. A scheduled reminder is a plan; a sent follow-up requires sending evidence. Drafting the sequence does not authorize sending or automation.

## Working agreement

Use context already supplied. Ask only for missing facts that change the next decision, preferably in one short batch, then make a useful first draft. Label assumptions and distinguish buyer evidence from your interpretation. Never invent interviews, prospect facts, results, testimonials, budget, or meetings. A target date is a planning goal, not a promised commercial outcome. Use plain sentences and normal hyphens in generated copy.

Create drafts and plans within the requested scope. Sending messages, booking meetings, publishing, changing a CRM, or committing the user to commercial terms requires authorization for that action. A request for advice alone does not authorize execution. Work from pasted notes if tools are unavailable; identify what remains unverified.

# Andrew Wilkinson Voice Corpus

Research date: 2026-10-18

The excerpts below are short quotations from public posts, articles, and talks, included under fair use for style analysis and attribution. They are third-party content and NOT covered by this repository's MIT license.

**Provenance note.** Items 1-40 are verbatim. Items 1-28 are copied byte for byte from essays on his own site, neverenough.com, most of them reprints of issues of his Never Enough newsletter (2016-2026); he writes these himself, with no ghostwriter credit anywhere on the site. Items 29-40 are his own X posts (@awilkinson), pulled directly from the X syndication timeline with his spelling and typos intact; where a post opens a thread, only the first tweet was captured and the truncation is marked (the Flow and burnout threads exist in full as essays, cited above). Items 41-44 are DOCUMENTED: YouTube auto-captions (ASR) of a two-and-a-half-hour interview on his own channel (May 2025), with YouTube-added punctuation and filler words retained. Nothing here comes from a quote aggregator. His 2024 memoir Never Enough was not fetched (no public extract), and no Tiny shareholder letter was located.

**Punctuation note.** Several essay excerpts preserve em dashes because they are verbatim quotations (items 3, 6, 7, 11, 13, 24, 26), and item 37 preserves an en dash used as an attribution mark; he also uses "→" arrows for sequences (item 4). The operator's copy rule still bans em and en dashes in anything generated in this voice; see the Punctuation Override in SKILL.md.

## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~13 words in essays, with frequent 2-5 word fragments; tweets average ~14 words per sentence and 2-6 sentences per post.
- One-line paragraphs: roughly 40% of essay paragraphs are one sentence or one fragment ("We were not.", "Problem solved."); X posts after a colon become one line per item.
- Hook-type frequency across the 40 written excerpts: confessional cold open with a number ~30%, flat contrarian claim ~25%, comic simile or prop ~15%, inversion or anti-goal ~15%, borrowed-authority anecdote (Buffett, Munger, a friend) ~15%.
- Fragment staircases: at least one per essay and in about a third of tweets ("Ten. Million. Dollars. Up in smoke. Money bonfire. GONE.").
- Comic similes with a specific prop: 1-3 per essay (popsicle in Death Valley, flagging down a plane, Fiji invading the United States); near zero on X.
- Exclamation marks: rare; only inside dialogue or one wink per essay ("bootstrappers, baby!"). Zero in the sampled tweets.
- Emoji: effectively zero (research notes a rare 🎈 or 🤯); hashtags: zero.
- Profanity: casual, 1-3 per long essay ("fuck off", "shit gets real", "shittier"); softened forms ("freaking", "crappy") more common; none aimed at a named person.
- Numbers: concrete and usually embarrassing. $10M lost, 40 percent success rate, $20k → $150k/month burn, $5,000 legal memo, 18 summers, 20 businesses / 400 employees / 80 investments.
- Hedges: "I think" and "kind of" appear in spoken answers; essays state conclusions and then undercut them with a self-own rather than a qualifier.
- Favorite openers: "This is the story of how I…"; a first-person admission plus a figure; "I hate…"; a flat claim ("Divorce is awesome."); on X, "How to win at business:" style colon-then-list.
- Favorite closers: a one-line paragraph that lands the joke or the lesson ("We were not.", "The ultimate.", "Try it sometime, it's insanely simple and strangely powerful."); a Buffett or Munger citation as final authority.
- Spoken register (ASR sample): "um" retained, "prisons of their own making", "lifestyle business", "20% better", "business is a really great hack to make friends"; same self-deprecation, slower and warmer than the essays.

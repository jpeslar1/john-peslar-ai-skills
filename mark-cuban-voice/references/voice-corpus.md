Every item below is sourced from a cited quote-archive URL (Shark Tank episode quote sites, interview aggregators such as graciousquotes.com, or Cuban's own company page) rather than a primary transcript or original recording fetched and confirmed by Claude this session. ## Derived stats (estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~13 words (estimate); spoken chains run longer.
- One-line paragraphs: he does not format for platforms; paragraphs follow speech, ~1-3 sentences (estimate).
- Hook-type frequency (estimate): effort gauntlets ~30%, blunt diagnoses ~25%, one-time truths ~15%, obsession tests ~15%, industry callouts ~15%.
- Emoji usage: near zero. ALL CAPS: occasional single word. Exclamation marks: occasional, when fired up.
- Favorite openers: CORRECTED 2026-10-18 from primary material: a rhetorical question he answers himself ("Does anybody here like their health care? ... That's why."), then direct second-person commands ("Do the work.", "Don't start...").
- Favorite closers: a hard practical point about effort or sales; the sardonic luck jab ("Then everyone can tell you how lucky you are."); "How cool is that."; "There are no shortcuts."; on Shark Tank, the clean walk-away.
- Pet phrases confirmed in his own typing (2026-10-18): "How cool is that.", "There are no shortcuts.", "do the work" / "done the work", "period, end of story", "7x24x365xforever", "Eat Your Lunch", "the sport of business"; "literally" as an intensifier and "I mean" / "you know" as connectives in speech. Typing quirks: a space before a question mark, "any where" as two words, typos left in.
- Effort doctrine wording (verified): plainer and more profane than the aggregator versions. "What a bunch of BS", "kick your ass", "Do the work." The polished one-liners in items 8-13 are not his phrasing.
- Spoken cadence (verified in two interviews): run-on with self-correction, chained with "and so", "but", "I mean", restarts mid-sentence, then a short declarative landing. Not clipped.
- 2026 lexicon shift: healthcare mechanics (PBMs, self-insured employers, "transparent companies", cash price, Medicare rates) and the recurring demand "show me a plan". He frames himself as the operator who lives it.
- Competition language: his own frame is "the sport of business" and "beat the hell out of yours". "Business is war" (item 16) is not his and is struck.

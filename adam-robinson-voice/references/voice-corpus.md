### Structural observations (verified across the 7 emails)

- No greeting, ever. Line 1 restates or escalates the subject and usually ends with a colon promise ("Here’s our full business plan (and how we get to $5m ARR):").
- ALL-CAPS section headers are standard newsletter furniture: "THE PRODUCT:", "THE TECH STACK:", "TAKEAWAY", "MONDAY - INTERNAL MEETING DAY:", plus caps self-objection headers voiced as the reader ("ALL THAT SOUNDS GOOD ADAM, BUT THAT BUSINESS SOUNDS “SMALL”."), answered in 1-4 words.
- Body is bullet-heavy: star/hyphen bullets for facts and stacks, numbered lists for steps and lessons, bolded one-line list headers.
- Multi-word caps runs mid-sentence are normal in email ("SINGLE BEST DISCOVERY", "ZERO PROGRESS", "SO BADLY"), not just single words.
- Closers accelerate: 3-6 one-line paragraphs stepping down to a recurring "keep building" beat, then the bare sign-off "Adam" alone on a line. No "Best," ever.
- P.S. carries the product plug with a social-proof number; nothing is sold mid-body.
- Emoji: effectively zero in these 7 emails. Profanity: present in about half, always as a verdict.

## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~11 words; jab sentences of 3-6 words are frequent ("Now, this is my art.").
- One-line paragraphs: an estimated 50%+ of LinkedIn post paragraphs; newsletters slightly longer.
- Hook-type frequency across sampled material: number flex ~35%, confession/self-accusation ~25%, milestone-plus-twist ~20%, fight-pick/contrarian ~20%.
- Caps usage: single emphasis words (BIG, NEVER) common; full-caps confessional hook lines occasionally.
- Stacked punctuation ("?!?", "???") appears in roughly 1 in 3 newsletter essays.
- Emoji: sparse; mostly sign-offs (✌️).
- Profanity: regular in newsletter essays (roughly once per essay), effectively absent from LinkedIn post bodies.
- Favorite openers: exact-number claims about himself; disbelief self-questions.
- Favorite closers: stated TAKEAWAY line, then a P.S. product plug with a social-proof number; newsletter signed "Adam".
- Numbers: hyper-specific everywhere (1,335 prospects, 700+ posts, 55M+ impressions, 6 people); specificity is the credibility mechanism.

# Gary Bencivenga Voice Corpus

Research date: 2026-10-19

The excerpts below are short quotations from public posts, articles, and talks, included under fair use for style analysis and attribution. They are third-party content and NOT covered by this repository's MIT license.

**Provenance note.** All 35 items are verbatim text Gary Bencivenga wrote himself. Items 1-32 are from Bencivenga Bullets (later Marketing Bullets), his complimentary e-letter of 29 issues, c. 2004-2008, read as the original static .htm pages captured by the Wayback Machine in January-February 2012 (26 of 29 issues pulled, 12 quoted; item 32 is the standing sign-off that closes every issue). Individual Bullets carry no dates; the series is bracketed by internal evidence (Bullet #23 opens on Gary Halbert's death in April 2007, every footer reads "Copyright 2008, Accountable Advertising, Inc."), so items are marked "undated (c. 2004-2008)". Items 33-35 are from the live marketingbullets.com homepage, his current first-person Marketing Maxims letter, fetched 2026-10-18. Nothing here comes from a quote aggregator; a third-party PDF compilation of the Bullets was seen but not used because the Wayback originals were available, and bencivengabullets.com is a JavaScript stub with no content. There is no documented-grade section because nothing spoken was retrievable. Not sampled: his spoken voice (the 2005 Bencivenga 100 seminar and his few interviews are paid or untranscribed), his actual control copy for Boardroom, Phillips and Rodale (quoted inside the Bullets only as headlines), and Bullets #4, #6-8, #10-11, #13-14, #17-18, #20-22, #24, #26, #28. He has no social channels, so the two-source footing is structural rather than a research shortfall.

**Punctuation note.** Items 13, 15, 19, 22, 27, 31, 33, 34 and 35 preserve unspaced em dashes ("Give good reasons for these three questions—why you, why true, why now?", "your most commonly encountered enemy—skepticism—is largely swept aside") because they are verbatim quotations; the em dash is his habitual device for the appositive and the reveal. The operator's copy rule still bans em and en dashes in anything generated in this voice; see the Punctuation Override in SKILL.md.

## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~19 words, with a deliberate alternation between a long courtly sentence (30-45 words, often a list of human wants or a stacked parable) and a short sealing line of 4-9 words ("Yeah, sure.", "In short, intention facilitates perception.", "That's it").
- One-line paragraphs: about 25% of the sampled paragraphs are a single sentence, almost always the moral or the reveal after a build ("A gifted product is mightier than a gifted pen.", "Puns in headlines are advertising malpractice.").
- Hook-type frequency across the 35 items: rule stated as doctrine (~30%: "Never make your claim bigger than your proof", "a single measurement is worth a thousand opinions"), named device or formula (~20%: I = B + C, the Credo Technique, the red shirts, Fuzzy Dice), parable or extended metaphor then one-line moral (~20%: horse and jockey, aspirin and headache, the deathbed whisper), split-run or test result as proof (~15%), lineage and borrowed authority (~15%: Halbert, Caples, Hopkins, Tom Collins, "the wise old salesman").
- Proof and numbers: a measurement, test or dollar figure in roughly a third of the items ("tens of millions of dollars in scientific direct response tests", "literally thousands of split-run tests", "handily beat the previous champ"); the structural habit is to withhold the winner, teach the principle, then reveal the result.
- Direct address: "Top Gun" as a mid-paragraph nickname in 3 of 35 items, with the fixed salutation "Dear Marketing Top Gun:" opening every issue (noted in the research, not quoted as an item); second person "you" in nearly every item.
- Exclamation marks: 6 across 35 items, confined to warm or playful moments ("I buy aspirin because I have a headache!", "I love clichés, and you should too!", "and (always!) higher response"), and he explicitly warns that overuse "increases the aroma of hype".
- Profanity: zero. Caps for emphasis: zero. Emoji, hashtags: zero (the channel predates them and the live homepage uses none).
- Diction: genteel and slightly archaic, with a wink ("wheeze my last breath", "kick the bucket (or bedpan)", "ogres for copy chiefs", "polished gem", "golden key", "toot my own horn", "like a charm"); clichés are used on purpose and defended ("They are clichés precisely because everyone already believes them").
- Metaphor density: an extended metaphor or parable in about half the items, often mixed knowingly (horse and jockey, rifle shot vs shotgun, gems, silk, gladiators of Rome, flesh and blood of your copy, a one-word university).
- Hedges: almost none on doctrine ("Never", "always", "There is no more powerful nor consistent way"), but self-praise is routed through others ("allow me to quote what others have said about my work") and admissions of early failure are frank ("reeked of mediocrity").
- Favorite openers: a credo or belief statement ("First, I believe the purpose of advertising is to sell"), a confession of early ineptitude, or a long comic set-up that delays the reveal ("If I were on my deathbed about to wheeze my last breath").
- Favorite closers: a one-line maxim that seals the lesson, an assignment to the reader ("Decide what it is, then start looking for it today. And don't close your eyes until you find it."), and the fixed sign-off "Sincere wishes for a good life / and (always!) higher response," followed on the original pages by a P.S. and P.P.S.
- Spoken vs written: no spoken record is in this corpus; every stat describes his e-letter and homepage letter only, and any claim about how he speaks is unsupported.

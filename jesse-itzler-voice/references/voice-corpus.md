# Jesse Itzler Voice Corpus

Research date: 2026-10-19

The excerpts below are short quotations from public posts, articles, and talks, included under fair use for style analysis and attribution. They are third-party content and NOT covered by this repository's MIT license.

**Provenance note.** Items 1-37 are verbatim. Items 1-19 are copied from the full bodies of Free Swim newsletter posts on his own site, jesseitzler.com (Jan 2025 to May 2026), pulled from the Shopify blog's Atom feed. One attribution caveat: the posts are first person and published under his name, but the Shopify author field reads "Brian Sutherland" (a staff account), and several read like cleaned-up transcriptions of his talks, so they are labelled own-newsletter, staff-posted, possible ghost or transcription. Items 20-37 are his own X posts (@JesseItzler, 2019-2025), pulled directly from the X syndication timeline and the fxtwitter single-post endpoint with his spelling and self-censored profanity intact; two are noted as word-for-word reposts of earlier posts. Item 38 is DOCUMENTED: the single line of an AI podcast transcript visible before a login wall, included only as the one spoken sample. Nothing here comes from a quote aggregator. Instagram, his highest-volume channel, is login-walled and was not fetched; the X posts appear to be the same captions. His books (Living with a SEAL, Living with the Monks) have no public excerpt and were not sampled.

**Punctuation note.** One newsletter excerpt (item 16) preserves an em dash because it is a verbatim quotation; the newsletter otherwise uses spaced hyphens for asides (items 1, 3, 4), and the X posts use "..." ellipses as the beat marker instead of any dash. The operator's copy rule still bans em and en dashes in anything generated in this voice; see the Punctuation Override in SKILL.md.

## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~10 words in the newsletter; X lines run 4-9 words, one beat per line, rarely more than 6 lines per post.
- One-line paragraphs: newsletter paragraphs are 1-3 sentences and the closer is almost always a one- or two-line paragraph; on X roughly half of all lines are a single sentence or fragment.
- Hook-type frequency across the 37 written excerpts: concrete scene with a count ("19 different couches", "Four miles.") ~30%, a named and capitalized rule or label (THE BUS RIDE, SIMPLE RULE, Kevin's Rule, Misogi) ~25%, flat imperative or CAPS maxim ~20%, mortality math ("Twenty-nine summers left.") ~15%, a question back to the reader ~10%.
- ALL CAPS: 1-3 capitalized words or one full-caps line in most X posts (READY FOR ANYTHING, PUT THEM ON THE CALENDAR, RELENTLESS FORWARD MOTION); near zero in the newsletter apart from a cross-posted BACKWARDS.
- Ellipses as the pause marker: in about a third of X posts ("Then one day...BOOM...the bus stops."); the newsletter uses a one-line paragraph for the same beat instead.
- Exclamation marks: rare in writing, 1 of 37 written excerpts ("And watch what happens!"); the one spoken line has three.
- Emoji: effectively zero in writing (one ✅ in item 32); research notes the set is limited to 👊 🙏 ✅. Hashtags: about a third of X posts close with one (#BuildYourLifeResume is the signature; also #EasyIsBoring, #NonNegotiable); zero in the newsletter.
- Profanity: self-censored only, 2 of 37 ("F#CK TRADITIONAL RESUMES", "epic sh#t"), plus one "Hell"; never fully spelled, never aimed at a person.
- Numbers: counts of time and reps rather than money or percentages: 19 couches, 29 summers, 16 visits, every 8 weeks, 1x a year, 5-year blocks, 18 feet by 10 feet, $375 a month, 6.2 mile swim; one percentage (90%) in the whole sample.
- Hedges: zero. No "I think", "maybe", or "it depends" anywhere in the written sample; claims are stated as rules.
- Favorite openers: a scene with a number ("When I turned 50", "Four miles. I couldn't finish a four-mile hike."); "Here's the thing"; a CAPS label followed by a period or colon ("THE BUS RIDE.", "SIMPLE RULE:"); "These 3 things will change your life:".
- Favorite closers: a two-line restatement of the rule ("This Saturday, not next winter. That's how you make sure regrets don't pile up."), a direct imperative ("Then follow the script.", "Don't let them down."), a time-rich reversal ("He looked time rich."), or a hashtag; never a summary.
- Spoken register: essentially unsampled; the one ASR line (item 38) is a greeting ("let's go ASAP!") and shows only that the spoken energy is higher and more exclamatory than the writing.

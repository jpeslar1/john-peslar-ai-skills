**Provenance note.** Items 1-33 are verbatim. Items 1-25 are copied from the full bodies of thirteen essays on nathanbarry.com (2016-2025), pulled through the site's public WordPress REST API; two original typos are preserved and flagged inline ("make sure don't" in item 9, "todays" in item 14) and are not voice features. His own podcast has no published transcripts, his books are paid and were not fetched, LinkedIn was not searched, and the X profile timeline was rate-limited so 2025-2026 posts beyond item 32 are unsampled.

**Punctuation note.** Two essay excerpts preserve em dashes because they are verbatim quotations (items 6 and 16), item 26 keeps his X ellipsis, and the essay titles cited for items 20 and 25 carry his own dashes. ## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~13 words in essays; X lines average 8-10 words; spoken sentences run 25+ words with "like" and "you know" as joints.
- One-line paragraphs: roughly 40% of essay paragraphs, rising to nearly all lines on X; one-word paragraphs appear at turns ("Nope.", "Holy shit.").
- Hook-type frequency across the 33 written excerpts: concrete scene or anecdote ~35%, surprising exact number ~30%, question to the reader ~15%, flat principle stated as a law ~10%, refusal or reversal ("I said no.") ~10%.
- Named frameworks: nearly every long essay introduces a capitalized term after the story (Ladders of Wealth Creation, Billion Dollar Creator, Creator Flywheels, Audience Shortcut, Single-Axis Fallacy, the Four Laws).
- Exclamation marks: effectively zero in the sampled essays and posts.
- Emoji: zero in the sampled corpus; hashtags: zero; @-mentions of his own company (@ConvertKit) in X posts.
- Profanity: rare and reserved for the peak beat ("Holy shit." at the $10M ARR screenshot); mild in speech.
- Hedges: few in writing; principles are stated flat ("Profit is leverage.", "What matters is who they are, not how many."); speech adds "I guess", "like", "probably".
- Numbers: exact and self-disclosing: $10,000,017 ARR, $1.2 billion, 99.8% net revenue retention, $6.9 million in profit sharing, $37,000 first month, less than 5% of annual revenue, 600 days in a row, 1,000 words a day.
- Credits: sources named inline (Jason Fried from Basecamp, Game of Thrones, Wendy's as his own starting point); he borrows and attributes rather than claims.
- Favorite openers: a question ("How many great TV shows have you discovered in season 3 or later?"); a scene ("You stare at the blank page"); a number that cannot be right ("This can't be right."); on X, a stat or refusal then a colon.
- Favorite closers: a two-line imperative maxim ("Create consistently. / Be patient for results."), a fragment that restates the thesis ("Even when that movement was a crawl."), or a direct wish for the reader ("That feeling is what I want for you.").
- Spoken register (ASR sample): same chronology and same exact figures ("600 days", "seven or $800,000", "$5 an hour work"), more self-deprecating, with "a very, very fancy way to say" undercutting the jargon.

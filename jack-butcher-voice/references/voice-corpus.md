# Jack Butcher Voice Corpus

Research date: 2026-09-09

The excerpts below are short quotations from public visualizevalue.com essays and the visualizevalue.com Visuals archive, included under fair use for style analysis and attribution. They are third-party content and NOT covered by this repository's MIT license.

**Provenance note.** Items 1-19 were read directly from visualizevalue.com essay pages carrying the byline "By Jack Butcher," or from the visualizevalue.com About page, on the research date, and are verbatim (`verified`). Items 20-29 are caption text pulled verbatim from the visualizevalue.com Visuals archive; the exact wording is confirmed against the live page, but standalone visual captions carry no byline or attribution on that page, so authorship is inferred from a strong pattern match to Jack Butcher's own confirmed essay titles or essay body text, not confirmed the way Items 1-19 are. These are labeled `documented`. See `source-map.md` for the full method and for the many Visuals-archive lines that were excluded because they turned out to be, or could not be ruled out as, quotations from other people (Alan Watts, Viktor Frankl, Lao Tzu, Carl Sagan, and others).

**Punctuation note.** A small number of excerpts below preserve an em dash because they are verbatim quotations, including one place where Jack Butcher himself uses an em dash mid-sentence in his own essay prose. The operator's copy rule still bans em and en dashes in anything generated in this voice; see the Punctuation Override in SKILL.md.

## Structural notes for the writer

- The title of the piece is very often also its last line, or its only line. "Slow Down" ends on "Slow down." "Make Yourself an API" repeats "Make yourself an API" mid-essay as its own paragraph. Treat the title as reusable inside the body, not just above it.
- Item 8 opens by quoting someone else by handle, then builds one short original argument on top of the quote. Crediting the source inline, not in a footnote, is part of the register.
- Numbered or bulleted lists carry no connective prose between items. Item 10's seven-line formula has zero transition words.
- The comma-pair and "X vs. Y" title pattern (items 21, 22, 23, 24, 26, 27) is the closest textual evidence available for how he compresses a contrast into two or three words for a caption. Treat it as his most identifiable structural signature.
- Definitions frequently arrive as a sentence fragment directly under a one-line header, with no verb connecting them (item 17 under item 16).

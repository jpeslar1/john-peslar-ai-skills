# Gary Vaynerchuk Voice Corpus

Research date: 2026-10-18

The excerpts below are short quotations from public posts, articles, and talks, included under fair use for style analysis and attribution. They are third-party content and NOT covered by this repository's MIT license.

**Provenance note.** Items 1-30 are verbatim, copied byte for byte from pages and timelines he publishes under his own name. Items 1-20 are from 2017 essays on garyvaynerchuk.com; each carries a footer crediting a staff writer ("This article was powered by Colin Campbell" or a Medium cross-post note), so they are his ideas and his rhythm with team assistance, and are labelled as such. Items 21-23 are from 2025-2026 posts on the same site with no assist credit (the Most Googled Questions shorts and a trading-card essay). Items 24-30 are his own X posts (@garyvee), pulled directly from the X syndication timeline with his spelling, spacing, and emoji intact; a handful of extra one-liners from the same pull sit below item 30. Items 31-34 are DOCUMENTED: YouTube auto-captions (ASR) of a keynote on his own channel; the source has no punctuation, masks profanity as [__], and contains ASR slips, so they are for cadence and stance, not exact wording. Nothing here comes from a quote aggregator. His weekly newsletter (email-gated, no archive) and his books were not sampled. The X sample skews 2018-2023; no 2024-2026 tweets were captured.

**Punctuation note.** Three excerpts preserve his own dashes because they are verbatim quotations: item 18 (an em dash before "Tweet This!") and items 22 and 23 (en dashes used as parenthetical pauses). On X he uses " .. " and " ... " where a dash would go. The operator's copy rule still bans em and en dashes in anything generated in this voice; see the Punctuation Override in SKILL.md.

## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~11 words in essays, ~16 words in tweets (most tweets are one or two sentences); spoken keynote runs in long unpunctuated chains of 30-60 words.
- One-line paragraphs: roughly half of essay paragraphs are one or two sentences; tweets are a single paragraph, sometimes with a blank-line turn before the second thought.
- Hook-type frequency across the 23 written essay excerpts: second-person indictment ~30%, aphorism pair or X-vs-Y label ~25%, death/mortality or time framing ~15%, credential or money receipt ~15%, question to the reader ~15%.
- Ellipses: his signature pause. In essays as "…" ("three little words… You're gonna die"); on X as a spaced " .. " or " … " in most tweets, often trailing the sentence.
- Exclamation marks: 1-3 per essay, doubled at peaks ("!!"); on X about one tweet in three ends on one.
- Emoji: near zero in essays (one 😉); on X a 💜 or a row of hearts closes roughly a third of tweets, plus 🤡 and ☀️ as punchlines.
- Caps for emphasis: one word per essay at most (SPEED, FREE, FEW); none on X, where lowercase starts and "u"/"pls" are the dialect.
- Profanity: casual and frequent. "fuck" or "shit" appears in about a quarter of the written sample and is masked [__] in the keynote captions; never aimed at a named person.
- Numbers: round and personal. Dollar figures ($65,000, $28,000, $150M), ages (34, 46, 92), and percentages used as rhetoric (99.9%, 98%); no citations, no sources.
- Hedges: effectively zero. No "might", "perhaps", or "in my opinion"; certainty is the register, softened only by "for me" and "I think" in the 2025 Q&A posts.
- Favorite openers: "Listen,"; "So…"; a flat diagnosis of the reader ("People are stuck because…"); on X, a lowercase imperative or a blessing ("If you see this tweet…").
- Favorite closers: a short dare or question ("What will you do?", "What's the alternative?"); a two-word imperative ("just start"); on X a trailing " .." plus a heart.
- Spoken register (ASR sample): call-and-response with the room, "I'm asking you desperately", "98% of you", "I have no emotion to where consumer's attention is"; the same indictment-then-dare structure as the essays, faster and more profane.

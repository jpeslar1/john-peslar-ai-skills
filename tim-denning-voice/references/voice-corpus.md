**Provenance note.** All 26 items are verbatim and written by him. Items 1-11 are copied from the full body_html of free weekly essays on timdenning.substack.com (August to October 2026), pulled through Substack's own archive and post API; the newest is three days old at research time. Items 12-22 are from full essay bodies on timdenning.com, pulled through the site's WordPress JSON endpoint (50 essays, 2024); these are the same pieces he published on Medium (identical titles in the Medium feed), so they stand in for his paywalled Medium register. Items 23-25 are from a 2020 LinkedIn Pulse article that renders logged out. Item 26 is a single X post fetched from the syndication endpoint with its ISO date. Nothing here comes from a quote aggregator. Not sampled: his short LinkedIn feed posts (no permalinks surfaced; profile authwalls), his X timeline beyond one post (429), Medium bodies (paywalled), and any spoken material, so every item is written prose. Asterisked profanity (f*ck, sh*t) is his own spelling, not a redaction.

**Punctuation note.** Item 25 preserves a spaced em dash because it is a verbatim quotation from the 2020 LinkedIn article; the 2024-2026 essays in this sample run on one-line paragraphs and full stops instead. ## Derived stats (honest estimates from this corpus)

- Median sentence length: ~9 words; a 20-30 word story sentence is routinely followed by a 1-4 word verdict ("Except f*cking me.", "No.", "Foolish.").
- One-line paragraphs: roughly 60 percent of essay paragraphs; the opening line and the key line of every piece sit alone ("I'm a millionaire, but most people in my life don't know.", "Readers buy humans, not niches.").
- Hook-type frequency across the 26 excerpts: flat personal confession ~30% ("I was a painful people-pleaser and a corporate drone."), contrarian verdict stated as fact ~25% ("Being rich is stupid.", "You can't think your way to confidence."), credential-by-numbers ~15% ("1B+ views & make 7 figures", "half a million followers"), announced provocation ~15% ("Let's stack up the hate comments right now.", "Sorry for saying this but it's true."), direct command or time-boxed promise ~15% ("Give me 4 mins. This will change your life.").
- Profanity: asterisked in roughly 1 of 4 excerpts (f*ck, sh*t), never spelled out; crude bodily imagery about as often ("polishing them all into a turd", "boy was my nose brown from all the poop").
- Caps for emphasis: zero in the 26 excerpts. Emoji: zero. Hashtags: zero, and explicitly mocked ("For the love of god. I'm begging you. No more hashtags.").
- Numbers: receipts and money figures in about 1 of 3 excerpts, rounded and brand-like (1B+ views, 7 figures, six-figures, $10,000, half a million followers, almost 10 years, 4 mins); no decimals and few percentages.
- Credential disclaimers: the brag is almost always followed by a shrug ("This is not a post designed to brag but to be helpful.", "This is my story and I'm proud of it.").
- Hedges: near zero. "It's not cool to say this, but" and "Sorry for saying this but it's true" are the only softeners, and both set up a harder claim.
- Rhetorical questions: about 1 in 6 excerpts, used as openers ("Is it just me or is the glamorous life overrated?") or as reader prompts ("Ask yourself: 'Why should someone click this?'").
- Favorite openers: a one-line confession with a number or a status ("I'm a millionaire", "Many of you think I write like sh*t."); a flat verdict on something everyone wants ("Everyone wants to be rich. Except f*cking me."); a declared genre ("This isn't a how-to post. It's a call to arms.").
- Favorite closers: a short command or dare ("Let's change that.", "Nail the headline or go home."), or a compressed aphorism in one line ("Polished is forgettable. Unhinged is never forgotten.", "Readers buy humans, not niches.").
- Register drift: the 2020 LinkedIn article is calmer, list-driven, and analogy-led (snowball, UX); the 2024-2026 essays are far more profane and story-led. Match the era the user wants.
- Spoken register: unsampled. All 26 items are written; treat any spoken-style output as inferred from the essay register.

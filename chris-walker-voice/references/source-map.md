## Public Positioning

Walker founded Refine Labs (demand-gen agency) and now leads Passetto, a GTM analytics and advisory firm. His public identity is one thesis repeated for years across LinkedIn, the State of Demand Gen / Revenue Vitals podcasts, and interviews: attribution software misleads, MQL-volume marketing destroys CAC, buyers discover vendors in "dark social" channels software cannot see, and the fix is self-reported attribution plus a KPI stack built on business outcomes (qualified pipeline, blended CAC, pipeline velocity, CAC payback).

The Passetto era (2024+) shifts the audience upward: the same argument reframed for CEOs, CFOs, and boards as a "GTM KPI stack" and "revenue factory" problem rather than a marketing-team problem.

## Distinct Chris Walker Patterns

### 1. One thesis, relentlessly

The same core argument re-cut through new datasets, customer numbers, and predictions for 6+ years. New posts are new angles on the standing thesis, never new thesis.

### 2. Data vindication

Claims are framed as already proven: "The same thing I've been saying for 6 years, now confirmed in a MASSIVE data set." Numbers are precise and slightly odd, with arithmetic derived in public.

### 3. Old Way vs. New Way

Binary contrast structures: lead gen vs. demand gen, software-based vs. self-reported attribution. The legacy playbook is timestamped to a specific year to declare it obsolete.

### 4. Ask the buyer

Self-reported attribution is the tiebreaker authority: "The most accurate way to get attribution is simply by asking them!"

### 5. Preemptive objection handling

The "p.s." disarms the anticipated counterattack before commenters raise it. Objections are reframed as outdated thinking rather than debated.

### 6. Escalation to the C-suite

Marketing problems are repositioned as CEO/CFO/board KPI-stack problems the marketing team cannot fix alone.

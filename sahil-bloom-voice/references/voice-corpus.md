## Structural notes for the writer

- Frameworks are always named before they are casually reused: "the Scarlet Robe Effect," "the Vasa Effect," "the Traffic Jam Theory of Success," "the Adjacent Possible," "the Bought Status Test."
- Real names are always attached to borrowed ideas: Leidy Klotz, Stuart Kauffman, Peter Drucker, Arthur Brooks, Lao Tzu, Morgan Housel, Helmuth von Moltke.
- The closing line frequently restates the essay's central claim in near-identical words to its first statement, creating a bookend.
- Fragment fragments ("New shoes. An elaborate wooden desk.") appear inside otherwise complete-sentence prose for rhythm, not confusion.
- The Friday Five format breaks from the long essay shape entirely: five short labeled mini-sections (Question, Quote, Framework, Routine, Visual) under one date header.
